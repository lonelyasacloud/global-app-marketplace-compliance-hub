## Country and region snapshot

| Market | Requirements currently listed by Google Play Help | Main impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brazil: merchant verification | Global merchants selling apps or in-app products in Brazil may need to provide business name/address, beneficial owners holding/controlling over 25%, executives, authorized representatives, address validation, and possibly date of birth or photo ID. Brazilian businesses also provide CPF/CNPJ. Google prompts affected accounts by email, Play Console, and Payments. | This is a payout/merchant verification issue; missing information may affect payments or account status. Do not check only Brazil-incorporated businesses. |
| Brazil: Digital ECA, age signals, and loot boxes | Google Help says apps aimed at or likely to be accessed by children/adolescents must ingest age-range data from app stores, and games in that scope may not offer loot boxes. The Play Age Signals API (beta) returns age ranges when the user or parent of a supervised user agrees to share. Google Play also blocks/filters 18+ apps/games for users determined to be minors. The IARC questionnaire may need resubmission for updated ClassInd ratings, especially for games with loot boxes. | Assess age-signal availability, consent, supervision, local classification, and Play Store filtering together. Age Signals does not replace the developer's own legal duties and must not be used for unrelated marketing/profiling. See [age restrictions and child safety](../age-restricted-child-safety/age-restricted-child-safety.en-US.md) and [content ratings](../content-ratings/content-ratings.en-US.md). |
| EU: geo-blocking | Apps distributed in the EU should not unjustifiably geo-block based on nationality, residence, or place of establishment. Google's example says a service should generally be accessible EU-wide, subject to other applicable laws; copyright-protected-content access as a service's main feature is listed as an exception. | Assess target markets, service type, copyrighted content, and statutory exceptions by market. Do not generalize this as an unconditional requirement to serve every EU region for every service. |
| Japan: trader information and financial/game rules | Paid apps or in-app purchases for Japanese consumers must display the operator's name, phone number, and physical address under the Specified Commercial Transactions Act. Accounts created before August 31, 2023 can maintain this in Console Account details/Developer page (or link a website with the details); newer accounts obtain the information from the selected Payments profile. Developers must also meet applicable laws such as the Payment Services Act; game developers may need to notify the local Finance Bureau and should consider protections for minors. | The account creation date changes the data source and maintenance path. Keep Console information aligned with the real entity and registrations. |
| South Korea: game ratings, randomized items, age verification, and location services | Games unsuitable for users under 19 require GRAC rating/licensing under the cited Korean rules; games with simulated gambling are treated as unsuitable for minors and need GRAC certification before distribution. Paid randomized virtual items must disclose odds before and close to purchase under Play Payments and Korean probability-item disclosure rules. Content designated “harmful to juveniles” by MOGEF is subject to Korean law and Play's age/real-name verification flow. Collecting location for location-based services may require KCC licensing, reporting service/collection/use/fee details, and user consent. | Simulated gambling, randomized items, mature content, and location services use different approvals/declarations; an IARC age label alone is not enough. |
| South Korea: developer contact details | Accounts created before August 31, 2023 use the Account Details/Developer page. Newer accounts maintain information through the developer-account/payment-profile flow. Korean developers selling paid apps or in-app products must provide the business-registration number, e-commerce license number, and issuing agency; Korean personal accounts must also verify and display a developer phone number. | The account creation date affects the workflow. Align entity, license, and Console records. |
| Israel: developer identity verification | Developers with an Israeli billing address must complete identity verification within Google's specified timeline. Individual/organization accounts exceeding 50,000 ILS over a rolling six-month period require enhanced KYC. Individuals provide ID; organizations provide registration and authorized-representative documents plus a hand-signed form, and schedule an approximately 10-minute Google Meet identity call. Failure may hold payments and restrict distribution/sales. | Follow the actual account email/Console alert and deadline; the public Help page does not establish one universal date for every account. |
| India: online games | Google Play says gaming apps distributed in India must comply with the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 and prohibits online money games as defined there; the page lists online social games and e-sports as outside that prohibition. | Treat the Play Help instruction seriously. The linked Act text, however, says in §1(3) that it commences on a date separately appointed by Central Government notification. This entry records Google's Play-facing instruction, not an independent legal conclusion that the Act is currently in force; check the Gazette commencement notice and subsequent legal status. |
| India: stock-trading/investment apps | Apps for stock trading, investing, or securities markets must comply with applicable SEBI rules. Google lists SEBI-verified badge conditions: complete the Financial features declaration, hold valid SEBI registration and recognized-exchange membership, appear in the relevant exchange's authorized mobile-app registry, and keep Console entity/contact details consistent. Developers must not misuse identifiers or imply regulatory endorsement beyond their scope. Google links BSE, NSE, MCX, and NCDEX registries. | Badge display criteria are not a complete licensing checklist. Verify the actual business model, licenses, and financial declarations. |
| Vietnam: online games | From December 25, 2024, Google's page points to online-game licensing and display-information requirements under Decree 147/2024/ND-CP. Developers should consult Vietnam's Authority of Broadcasting and Electronic Information (ABEI) and its licensing lookup/portal, and keep digital copies of licenses. | The license category depends on game/service type. Google's page says not to rely on its summary alone for Vietnamese legal compliance. |

## Age Signals technical descendants

The Play Age Signals API [overview](https://developer.android.com/google/play/age-signals/overview) and [usage guide](https://developer.android.com/google/play/age-signals/use-age-signals-api) are technical pages linked from the Brazil section. At review, Android documentation showed SDK 0.0.4; the API first returns `SHARED`, `NOT_SHARED`, or `VERIFICATION_REQUIRED`, and the app requests age data only after a shared status. Default bands are 0–12, 13–15, 16–17, and 18+, with custom ranges possible. The app does not receive age signals after refusal. In regions requiring age verification, a user may need to resolve verification/supervision in the Play Store. The API guide also describes mandatory age-sharing flows in some US states, so this technical branch is not Brazil-only. Limit use under Play Data/User Data rules and applicable law. The API's sharing preferences, response fields, significant changes, revoked approvals, and user-facing controls are now expanded in the [Age Signals technical branch](../age-restricted-child-safety/age-restricted-child-safety.en-US.md).
