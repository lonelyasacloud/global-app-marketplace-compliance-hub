## Requirements

Google Play offers Personal and Organization accounts. Financial, health, `VpnService`, and government apps must use an Organization account. Keep legal name/address, contact details, applicable public developer contacts, payment methods, and linked Payments Profile accurate. Organization accounts generally need a D-U-N-S number (government organizations may have an exception) and must keep details aligned with Dun & Bradstreet. See the [health apps and Health Connect topic](../health-apps-and-health-connect/health-apps-and-health-connect.en-US.md) for health-specific declarations and data rules.

### Verify developer identity information

[Verify developer identity information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10841920?hl=en) distinguishes public developer information from private contact information used by Google. All developers verify the developer email shown on Play; Organization accounts also verify the public developer phone. Developers separately verify the contact email and phone Google uses to reach the account owner; those contacts are not shown to Play users. Updating a phone number or email generally requires reverification.

Identity checks rely on a linked Google Payments Profile. If it is unverified, an individual generally provides a government ID; an organization generally provides a D-U-N-S number, government ID, and official organization document. Known government organizations may receive a D-U-N-S exception; applying for D-U-N-S is free. Identity documents must match the Payments Profile, and accepted documents vary by country/region. Organizations verify a website, while individuals complete device verification, before the related phone verification. Korean Personal accounts have an additional public phone requirement. Developers can start verification 60 days before their individual deadline. If information cannot be verified, the developer presence and apps may be removed, and republishing may be blocked until verification is complete.

The English Help page was accessible in this review; the Chinese URL was not reliably accessible. This is a bilingual paraphrase of the English page, not a substitute for the account's Console tasks and local document list.

Before submission, provide accurate app metadata, a privacy policy, Data safety details, and working review credentials/resources such as a demo account and QR code. Register the package name under Android developer-verification requirements. The app, ads, analytics, and SDKs must follow Developer Program Policies; child-targeted apps must also follow Families requirements and offer stable functionality.

### Requesting more time for a declaration

[Request more time to complete a policy declaration](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15644220) says that only selected, complex declarations offer extensions. In Play Console, open the relevant declaration under **App content**, choose “Request additional time,” select the applicable reason, and submit. If granted, the new deadline appears in that declaration's Timeline. Each policy has a fixed extension period; not every declaration offers one, and completed or out-of-scope declarations generally do not show the option. An extension changes the completion date, not the declaration or compliance duty, and should not be treated as automatic approval.

The article's direct links include signed-in [App content](https://play.google.com/console/app/app-content/summary) and [Prepare your app for review](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859455). Their other declaration/review descendants are inventoried above. The extension article separately shows 27 Help Center sidebar recommendations; they are not body links and are not counted as part of the extension workflow. The Console form and each app's eligibility/new deadline require account access.

### Testing gates for new Personal accounts

- **Production access:** Personal developer accounts created after November 13, 2023 must run a closed test before applying for production access. At least 12 testers must remain opted in continuously for 14 days; then answer questions about the test, app design, and production readiness in Play Console. If testers opt out and later rejoin, the continuous period restarts. This specific gate does not apply to Organization accounts.
- **Device verification:** New Personal accounts must use the Play Console mobile app to verify access to a real Android device. The official article specifies a non-rooted physical Android device running Android 10 or higher.
- Sources: [App testing requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465), [Device verification requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14316361). Requirements can vary by account type and creation date.

The device-verification Help article's body steps link only to the signed-in Play Console Home. In practice, the account owner scans a QR code from the web Console, then selects the same developer account in the Play Console mobile app and completes verification. The article has 29 Help Center sidebar recommendations, inventoried separately from body links; the account task and verification result require sign-in.

### Current 2026 target API deadline

As of 2026-10-24, the annual Google Play target API deadline passed on August 31, 2026. New apps and updates generally need Android 16/API 36; Wear OS and Android Automotive OS need API 35; Android TV and Android XR need API 34. Existing-app minimums for discoverability to new users on newer devices are, respectively: general apps API 35, Wear OS API 34, Android TV API 33, Android Automotive OS API 32L, and Android XR API 34. Only impacted apps receiving the relevant noncompliance warning can request an extension to **November 1, 2026** from the Play Console warning/issue details. Check the account-specific notice and [official target API requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/11926878). ## Account transfers

Use Google Play’s formal ownership-transfer process; both parties are responsible during the transition. Unauthorized account sales, leases, or purchases are prohibited. To move only an app, use the separate app-transfer process.

### Current ownership-transfer flow and eligibility

The Play Console Help article [Transfer ownership of a developer account](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16909862?hl=en) was reopened in full on October 24, 2026. Only the current account owner can designate an existing user through **Users and permissions → Manage → Make account owner**. For an organization account, the prospective owner's relationship (same or new organization) must be specified: a same-organization user can reuse existing account information; a user from a different entity selects a payments profile for identity verification; and for an in-organization transfer the prospective owner must have admin access to the required payments profiles. For an individual account, the prospective owner selects or creates a payments profile used only for identity verification. After the hold, the setup wizard also collects organization type/size, organization phone and optional website, contact name, OTP-verified contact email/phone, and developer contact phone/email. Google may separately request identity documents; unsupported documents are rejected.

| Eligibility or step | Current Help-page rule |
| --- | --- |
| Directly eligible | Organization developer accounts and non-monetizing Personal accounts may transfer ownership after required information and applicable identity checks are complete. |
| Not eligible for direct transfer | A Personal developer account monetizing apps cannot currently change its owner through this flow; create a new developer account and transfer the apps separately. |
| Seven-day security hold | After submission, the current owner and admins with access to all apps receive email, and team members receive a Console notice. A 7-day hold follows; any account user can cancel. After it expires, the prospective owner finalizes through an email invitation; once they begin the final process, other users can no longer cancel. |
| Possible blocks | Outstanding verification or compliance issues; a pending Payments Profile change or account-type transition; a pending upload-key reset or app transfer; or a prospective owner who already owns another Play Console account. |
| Current owner unreachable | Self-service transfer is not currently available; contact Support through Play Console Help or the public online form. |

This is an account-level operation, separate from [transferring an individual app](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247?hl=en). The April 15, 2026 [policy announcement](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16926792) lists the new Account Transfer policy; its [official blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/04/giving-users-clearer-choice-and-everyone-a-safer-more-trusted-app-ecosystem.html) explicitly says ownership changes must use the official feature starting **May 27, 2026**. Unofficial transfers through credential sharing or third-party marketplaces are not permitted. ### App transfer: prerequisites and effects

The body of [Transfer apps to another developer account](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247?hl=en) was opened. Both source and target accounts must be valid, the app must comply with policy, and developers should first download bulk export, payment, and earnings reports they will need later. The target account's registration transaction ID is required. After the recipient reviews and accepts the request, the Help page currently says Support usually responds within two business days.

| Transfer item or exception | Operational effect |
| --- | --- |
| Moves with the app | Users, download statistics, ratings/reviews, content rating, store listing, subscriptions, and Android developer-verification package names/signing keys; submitted policy declarations and App content information may also move. |
| Does not move | Bulk export, estimated-sales and earnings reports; promotions; open/closed/internal test groups and internal app sharing; integration permissions/associations. Recreate closed tracks in the new account; testers may need to opt in again. |
| Orders and refunds | Orders created before transfer remain in the source account. Refund them there or use the Google Play Developer API. |
| Play App Signing | The target account may request a new upload key; it can also request an app-signing-key upgrade for new installs. Check the effect on the app's current signing setup first. |
| Paid apps and in-app products | The target account needs an active Payments Profile. A default-currency change may unpublish an in-app-product app until prices are checked; paid-app prices may be adjusted automatically. |
| External services | Update Google Analytics, Google Cloud projects, Firebase, ad SDKs (including AdMob), and other integrations. Complete translation work first. Private enterprise apps have organization and transfer restrictions; apps created through the enterprise iframe cannot be transferred. |
| 15% service-fee account groups | If accounts belong to different groups, the transferred app's current-year earnings may be counted in both the source and target groups. This depends on account-group/fee-plan status; current fees are controlled by the current policy and Console. |

### Managed Google Play private apps: body and best-practice descendants

Managed Google Play private apps are not listed in the consumer-facing public Play Store and are available only to users in specified organizations. They can be published through the Managed Play iframe, Play Console, or Custom App Publishing API; each app can be associated with up to 1,000 organizations. Making a private app public generally requires a new package name; apps created through the iframe cannot be transferred to another developer account. The target API exception for apps that are permanently private and intended only for internal organizational use is separate from Android developer verification's managed-device scope; see the Android developer-verification topic (snapshot: `android-developer-verification-en.md`, not imported in this repository).

## Review status and appeals

Check **Policy status** in Play Console for rejection, removal, suspension, and reasons. A rejection generally leaves the last successfully published version available; removal takes the app down; suspension makes it unavailable. Do not resubmit the same rejected/removed build before fixing the issue. If you believe the decision is wrong, appeal through the notice or Console. See [Check your app’s policy status](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9842754).

For the operational sequence across test tracks, permission declarations, review submission, managed publishing, and staged rollouts, see [Google Play Testing, Review, and Release Workflow](../publishing/play-release-workflow.en-US.md).
