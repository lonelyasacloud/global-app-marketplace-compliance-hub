## Purpose and applicability

The Data safety section helps users understand before installation whether an app collects or shares user data and what security practices it uses. Developers complete the form under App content in Play Console. Google reviews it as part of app review, and the resulting information appears on the Google Play listing.

Every developer with an app published on Google Play must complete a form for each app package, including apps on closed, open, and production tracks. Apps active only on internal testing may be exempt. System services and private apps do not need the form. Apps that collect no data still need to complete the form and provide a privacy-policy link. Some legacy target-SDK artifacts may be excluded under the conditions in the Help article.

The developer alone is responsible for accurate and complete declarations. Google does not determine data practices on the developer’s behalf. A mismatch between behavior and disclosures can lead to required remediation, blocked updates, or removal. Data collected or shared by SDKs and libraries must be included; developers need to verify the technical details with providers.

## How to classify collection and sharing

### Collection

Collection generally means transmitting user data off the user’s device. Consider:

- SDKs and libraries, whether data goes to the developer’s server or a third-party server.
- A WebView whose code or behavior is controlled by the app. Open-web browsing by the user is generally outside this WebView rule.
- Pseudonymous data that can reasonably be associated with a user.

Data processed only on the device and never transmitted is not collected for this purpose. Data sent off-device using end-to-end encryption can also qualify for an exception when neither the developer nor an intermediary can read it and only the sender and recipient hold the necessary keys.

The general rule says off-device data processed ephemerally still needs to be included in the developer's form response, although it will not appear in the Play listing when it meets the definition. The consumer-facing Help article also says ephemeral-only data need not be disclosed as “collected” in the public Data safety section, which describes the same display outcome and does not waive the form response. A specific FAQ on later server logging says the qualifying transient use need not be included in the form response. The wording tension within the developer article remains; see below. Any use beyond ephemeral processing must still be declared.

### Sharing

Sharing means transferring data collected from the app to a third party. It includes server-to-server transfers, on-device transfers to another app, direct SDK transfers, and transfers through an app-controlled WebView.

Under qualifying conditions, the official article excludes from “sharing” certain transfers to a service provider acting on the developer’s instructions, transfers for specific legal purposes, user-initiated transfers the user reasonably expects, transfers following compliant prominent disclosure and consent, and fully anonymized data that cannot be linked to a person. Classification should not be based only on who owns the server.

The “first party” is the organization primarily responsible for processing app data, usually the publisher named on the Play listing. A service provider processes data on its behalf. Other organizations are generally third parties.

For each data type actually collected or shared, answer whether it is collected, shared, processed only ephemerally, required/optional, and why it is used. Official purpose choices and definitions are:

| Purpose | When it applies (summary of the official definition) |
| --- | --- |
| App functionality | Supports features available in the app, such as enabling a feature or authenticating users. |
| Analytics | Measures app use or performance, such as feature usage, app health, diagnosing/fixing bugs, or future performance improvements. |
| Developer communications | Sends news about the app or developer, such as a security update or new-feature notification. |
| Advertising or marketing | Shows/targets ads or marketing communications, or measures ad performance; examples include in-app ads, promotional push messages, or sharing data with ad partners. |

| Personalization | Customizes app content or recommendations, such as playlists based on listening habits or local news based on location. |
| Account management | Creates, signs in, verifies, or manages a user account and information provided by the developer. |

Declare data as optional only if all users, across devices and regions, can choose whether to provide it or can opt in/out. If any currently distributed version requires the data, the overall declaration must say it is required; do not classify solely by the newest version.

## Security practices and optional badges

- Developers may declare encryption in transit. This can be claimed for the app only when all user data transmitted off-device by the app and its SDKs/libraries is encrypted in transit.
- State whether users can request deletion of their data; answer accurately in the form.
- Apps for child audiences that have reviewed compliance with Families requirements may opt in to the Families commitment badge.
- Developers may pay an authorized Google lab to assess an app under MASA/OWASP MASVS and then opt in to an independent security-review badge. The assessment may not verify the accuracy or completeness of Data safety disclosures; the developer remains responsible.
- Apps using India’s UPI transfer system may be eligible to declare the UPI badge.

## Completing and maintaining the form

1. Inventory data flows across the app, all versions, SDKs/libraries, servers, WebViews, permissions, and APIs.
2. Prepare a privacy policy; review the Data safety requirements and User Data policy.
3. In Play Console → App content → Data safety, answer collection/sharing, purpose, optionality, and security questions for each data type.
4. CSV export can help with review or backup. Importing a CSV overwrites answers already in the form, so preserve the current state first.
5. After submission, monitor review messages and Policy status. Update the form when features, data flows, SDKs, purposes, or recipients change, and keep it consistent with the privacy policy.

## Key interpretations and common mistakes

- A permission in the manifest does not by itself establish collection for Data safety; determine whether data leaves the device and how it is processed. The permission policy applies separately.
- If the app intentionally derives or collects another attribute from a data type (for example, inferring race/ethnicity from photos), declare the derived category too.
- Account information can be declared for account management; declare other app-specific purposes when the app uses the information for them.
- If a user uploads directly to their own external cloud storage and the app never collects or accesses the content, the app may not need to declare that collection; verify the implementation.
- There is no single required mechanism for deletion requests, but it should be easy to discover and use. Account deletion must also meet the in-app and external requirements in the [User Data policy](../user-data/user-data.en-US.md).
- Data safety is distinct from the system-generated permissions list: one describes data collection/sharing; the other reflects install-time permissions in the manifest.
- An optional security-review badge does not certify the accuracy of every privacy declaration.

## FAQ, review, and boundary questions

### Submission, review, and listing updates

- The FAQ's 2022 grace-period answer is historical and is not a current extension rule. Inaccurate declarations can require remediation; failure to comply may block updates or lead to removal. Google review does not verify every data flow on a developer's behalf and does not replace review under other Developer Program policies.
- Review time varies. Some apps receive expanded review, which may take up to seven days or longer in exceptional cases. If a listing has not changed, check Publishing overview for a pending review, then check whether Managed publishing requires a manual release. Refresh the listing after publication; device delivery may take several days and sometimes up to seven days.
- Google Play's data taxonomy may differ materially from iOS privacy frameworks, so an iOS disclosure cannot simply be copied over. Data safety helps users decide whether to install; it does not by itself decide whether a data practice is appropriate. User Data, permissions, and other policies still govern access and use. Update the form when practices change; prior approval is not permanent protection from later enforcement.

### Form scope, consent, and classification

- Each package name has one global form. It represents data practices across all versions, regions, and age groups currently distributed on Google Play. If any currently distributed version collects or uses a data type, the global form must reflect it; if any version requires that data, do not mark collection optional. Version-specific notes can go in “About this app.”
- The listing's Data safety section does not add a new disclosure or consent prompt to installation. In-app prominent disclosure and consent required by the existing User Data policy remain separate obligations.
- A manifest permission alone does not establish actual collection; declare data only when it is collected or shared, while still complying separately with sensitive-permission rules. If the app purposefully derives one data type from another, disclose both. Pseudonymous data remains in scope when it can reasonably be linked back to a user.
- Classify IP addresses, account names, advertising IDs, and event IDs by their actual use and whether they relate to an identifiable user. Account details commonly relate to account management; add specific purposes such as app notifications or personalization when the app itself uses the data that way.

### Deletion, providers, and security practices

- The form requires an answer about whether users can request deletion, but there is no prescribed implementation. The Help article recommends an easily found channel such as an in-app feature, contact form, or dedicated email address. The badge may be selected when users can request deletion or when deletion/anonymization is automatically initiated within 90 days of collection. Legitimate retention for legal compliance or abuse prevention does not necessarily prevent selecting it. This badge does not replace the separate account-deletion policy.
- The global-form FAQ reiterates that practices across all regions and versions must be represented, but it does not give a more specific answer for a deletion mechanism unavailable to some regions. This page does not infer a Console answer; check the current form or ask Play support based on actual regional coverage.
- A service provider may qualify for the sharing exception only when it processes data on the developer's behalf and instructions. An SDK that builds advertising profiles across customers is not acting solely as the developer's service provider and should be assessed as sharing. Server ownership alone does not determine the result.
- A payment provider may collect card details without the app declaring that collection if it obtains them directly under its own terms and the app never accesses them. Still inventory purchase history, fraud signals, and other financial data received by the app. A direct upload to a user's own cloud drive may also fall outside app collection when the app never accesses the content and the upload is governed by the storage provider's terms.
- Use current industry-standard encryption in transit such as TLS/HTTPS. Anonymization depends on the use case and should be assessed with privacy/security experts; Google's differential-privacy page is an example, not a Play-mandated technique.
- **Anonymization example branch (bilingual recheck, October 24, 2026):** Google's [anonymization explainer](https://policies.google.com/technologies/anonymization?hl=en) describes generalization (including k-anonymity and l-diversity) and adding mathematical noise through differential privacy; it notes that noise can reduce data utility. These are examples of Google's methods, not a Play-prescribed algorithm, threshold, or validation standard. The Play FAQ's outcome test remains whether data is fully anonymized and cannot be associated with an individual; naming a technique does not prove an app's data meets that result. The embedded video returned Cache miss when clicked; Google/Privacy/Terms footer links are generic site navigation, not Play rules. In the account-deletion branch, the 90-day automatic deletion/anonymization condition affects the Data safety badge answer only; it does not exempt an account-creating app from the in-app and external account-deletion paths.
- System services are preinstalled software supporting core system functions and may apply for a form exemption. Do not assume every preinstalled app is automatically exempt.

### Ephemeral processing: scope of the general rule and the specific FAQ

The official English Help article's general rule says off-device data meeting the ephemeral definition must be included in the **form response**, although it will not appear in the Play listing's Data safety section. The FAQ asks about a narrower lifecycle: data first serves a real-time page/client request and is only later written to server logs and used for other purposes. It says the earlier transient servicing use need not be included, but all later uses of the data, including purposes for logged data, must still be declared.

**Scope reading (the stages can be partly reconciled; Console field encoding still needs confirmation):** The FAQ's “this use” can be read as excluding only the earlier real-time servicing purpose, not the later logging, analytics, profiling, or other non-ephemeral uses. It is not a general exception for every data type that was briefly processed. A pure ephemeral case with no later retention differs from the FAQ's example of data that is subsequently logged/used: the general rule still says qualifying off-device ephemeral processing is included in the form response but hidden from the public listing. **CSV workflow clarification (October 24, 2026):** The Help article says to select the data types an app collects or shares first, then answer usage/handling questions for each selected type. Its rendered Name example places `PSL_DATA_USAGE_EPHEMERAL` in that per-type question group, with sample response `TRUE` and requirement `MAYBE_REQUIRED`. This confirms that the question belongs to a conditional, per-data-type workflow rather than a global form field. The article still does not name which previous answer triggers this field's `MAYBE_REQUIRED` condition (for example, selecting the type, choosing collected/shared, or another condition), whether a purely ephemeral type must be selected, or how to answer when the same type is transiently served and later retained. **The exact field trigger remains unpublished**; do not treat the example as a universal answer or this workflow inference as Console instructions.

| Data lifecycle | What the official text supports | Form boundary to verify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Processed only on-device and never transmitted | Outside the article's off-device “collection” definition. | If an SDK/server path also transmits the data, classify that actual flow separately. |
| Transmitted off-device and used only for one real-time request, held only in memory, then deleted | Under the definition, the general rule says include it in the form response but omit it from the listing. | The workflow only says to answer processing questions for each data type declared as collected/shared; it does not say whether purely ephemeral data must first be selected as a type. |
| Used transiently for a real-time request, then logged or used for another purpose | The FAQ excludes the earlier transient servicing use but requires reporting later uses and the purposes for logged data. | The field appears in the per-type question group, but the article does not say which collected/shared answer or other prior answer makes it mandatory. |
| Used for profiling, ad analytics, or retention/analysis after the request | Cannot be treated as ephemeral alone; disclose based on the actual data type, collection/sharing path, and purpose. | If advertising is involved, also check the Ads policy and other applicable restrictions. |

### Cross-check against Android app metadata (platform representation, not a ruling on Play form fields)

Android's official [app metadata overview in English](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/features/app-metadata?hl=en) and [Simplified Chinese version](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/features/app-metadata?hl=zh-cn) say that apps distributed through Google Play enter this information through the Play Console Data safety form. The app metadata bundle is the Android installer/platform-readable representation of data safety information that can be shown to users. It is separate from legal transparency and disclosure obligations in the developer's operating jurisdictions.

The [English schema reference](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/features/app-metadata-schema) and [Simplified Chinese route](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/features/app-metadata-schema?hl=zh-cn) define `ephemeral` as a Boolean field for server-side processing that stays in memory, is not written to disk, and is retained no longer than necessary for the specific request. The schema explicitly limits this field to `data_category` bundles representing **data collection**, not data sharing. The Android metadata overview repeats the narrower “real-time request, then later logged” case: the transient servicing stage can be omitted from the metadata bundle, while later logged-data uses and purposes still need to be declared.

**Boundary with the Play form:** These pages add the Android platform representation and the field's scope, and confirm that Play-distributed apps use Play Console to enter their information. They do not specify a one-to-one mapping between the Android schema's `ephemeral` Boolean and the Play form's `PSL_DATA_USAGE_EPHEMERAL` response, and they do not resolve the Play Help article's tension between its general rule and FAQ about inclusion in the form response. Keep the Play form response, platform metadata representation, and consumer-facing listing distinct; do not treat the schema field as a Console instruction.

### Private-app exemption: definition, distribution descendants, and scope

On October 23, 2026, we retraced the private-app exemption link in the Data safety article. The old [support.google.com/a/answer/2494992](https://support.google.com/a/answer/2494992) URL now redirects to [Manage private Android apps in Google Play](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/devices/manage-private-android-apps-in-google-play?hl=en), last updated October 20, 2026; its current body is readable. It describes a private app as an internal-work Android app restricted to a specified organization and explains Admin console / Play Console publishing, organization-unit or group access, and device-management prerequisites. It also says an app cannot be both a private app and a public Play app at the same time, and private apps do not support billing. Adding a public app to an organization's Managed Play app list does not make it private.
