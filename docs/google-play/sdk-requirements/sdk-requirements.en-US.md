## Core responsibilities

- Identify the actual permissions, accessed data, recipients, and purposes of every direct and transitive SDK. Data collected by default in an SDK must be considered even when the app does not call a particular feature.
- SDK behavior must comply with Play’s User Data, sensitive-permissions, malware, device/network-abuse, Families advertising, Ads, and deceptive-behavior requirements. Responsibility does not move to the vendor just because a third party collects or receives the data.
- Share user data with a third party only when needed for the app’s disclosed functionality. Verify that public SDK documentation describes the exact integrated version and its actual behavior.
- When an SDK is designed to collect personal or sensitive data by default, the app must meet applicable prominent-disclosure and consent requirements and ensure the SDK follows the user’s choice. If Google Play requests evidence, the usual response period is **two weeks**, unless the request gives a longer period.
- Do not sell personal or sensitive user data. Protect transmission with modern encryption and do not associate persistent device identifiers with sensitive or resettable identifiers without a listed exception.

## High-risk prohibited behavior

| Risk | Checks and response |
| --- | --- |
| Undisclosed or noncompliant data collection | Inventory permissions, data types, recipients, purposes, retention, and sharing. Keep Data safety and the privacy policy current. Ask the vendor for clear evidence or remove an SDK that cannot be assessed. |
| SDKs in child-directed apps | Apps targeting children may only integrate SDKs that meet Families requirements. Child ad content, data handling, and targeting have additional restrictions. As of October 23, 2026, the Families Self-Certified Ads SDK page says it is not accepting new applicants; existing participants remain subject to current program rules. Recheck the current list and rules before every integration. |
| Malware, spyware, or opaque behavior | SDKs must not steal credentials, evade Android’s permission model, secretly collect or transmit data unrelated to compliant functionality, obstruct uninstall, disable SELinux, or use hidden features to evade review. |
| Unauthorized downloads or self-updates | An SDK must not download executable code outside Google Play, install apps from unknown sources, or update its own APK. Runtime-loaded interpreted code must not introduce policy violations or security vulnerabilities. |
| Unsafe WebView/network code | Do not let a WebView with a JavaScript interface load untrusted content or URLs; do not download files over insecure connections or expose users to vulnerabilities. |
| Undeclared foreground services | If an SDK uses a foreground service, it needs an appropriate use case and the merged app manifest must declare it correctly. The SDK’s use must also meet Play policy. |
| Deception or review evasion | SDKs must not impersonate system UI, hide or defer functionality, mislead users, or change behavior based on review status to evade review. |

## SDK risk signals and remediation windows

Google Play flags some known issues with popular SDKs in Play Console. Notices may appear on the Production or Release overview page. New app releases using an SDK version that causes a policy violation may be rejected. When an SDK owner reports a version as outdated or having notable issues, the Help article describes a warning and recommends following the owner’s guidance. For a version with a **critical issue**, the article permits a temporary release but requires updating to a newer version **within 90 days of that release**. After that window, further app releases using the affected version are blocked. Follow the actual account notice, dates, and remediation instructions.

The [Google Play SDK Index](https://developer.android.com/distribute/sdk-index) provides signals for common commercial SDKs, including usage, version adoption, requested permissions, retention, and data-safety guidance; Google’s documentation says it covers more than 100 widely used commercial SDKs. Its Help article limits eligibility to commercial Maven SDKs in an existing category that meet the usage thresholds (used by 100 apps and at least 100 million app installs). The data comes only from apps that share dependency data with Google Play and have more than 1,000 installs; installs count on devices used at least once in the past 30 days. Absence from the Index therefore does not mean an SDK is unused or exempt.

Listings may show a provider-registration badge, Maven artifacts, latest version/API requirements, adoption by install-base size, the five most-used versions, Android permissions used by an SDK version published within the past year, retention probability, provider version messages, and optional Data safety guidance. Artifact lists may be incomplete, and Google Play says it cannot always detect every permission an SDK uses. A registration badge means the provider registered in SDK Console; it is not a policy-compliance certification. Permission signals can help identify restricted-access or disclosure/consent requirements, but developers must still inspect the merged manifest, actual SDK version, and app data flows. The interactive [SDK Index at play.google.com/sdks](https://play.google.com/sdks) returned only an embedded shell in this review, so the offline knowledge base does not contain a live, per-SDK catalog.

Android build documentation explains how this index relates to release diagnostics: [AGP adds dependency metadata to build artifacts](https://developer.android.com/build/dependencies), which Play Console can inspect for known SDK/dependency issues; Android Studio also shows lint warnings for indexed SDKs marked outdated, policy-violating, vulnerable, or deprecated. The metadata supports diagnosis by default; the build configuration allows opting out of including it in APKs/AABs, though Android recommends keeping it. This is Android build implementation guidance, not a new Play policy obligation. Use the app/version-specific Play Console notice and deadline for remediation.

## Integration and update checklist

1. Export direct and transitive dependency details; record SDK name, exact version, source, owner, update path, and known issues. Inspect the dependency file in the delivered AAB/APK, not only the source declarations.
2. Before integration, read the version’s public data-safety and permission documentation. Check initialization defaults, network endpoints, persistent identifiers, logging, ads, and background behavior. Use the SDK Index and vendor advisories as supporting evidence, not as the only evidence.
3. Map SDK permissions to merged-manifest entries, promoted app features, and Play Console declarations. Remove permissions or SDKs without a legitimate feature purpose.
4. Apply user choices before an SDK accesses sensitive data or starts collection. The SDK should read app preferences, or the app should initialize it after consent and pass the consent state. Verify that refusal or withdrawal stops disallowed processing.
5. Include SDK collection/sharing, purposes, security, and deletion practice in Data safety. Keep the privacy policy and any applicable in-app prominent disclosure consistent about recipients, data types, and purposes.
6. For child-directed apps, verify that the SDK and version still meet Families requirements and that the self-certified ad SDK list applies. A vendor’s “child-directed” mode does not remove the need to verify ad creatives, data behavior, and mediation settings.
7. Recheck permissions, network behavior, disclosures, Data safety, and review paths whenever an SDK version, initialization option, data flow, ad provider, or analytics provider changes. Monitor Console notices and meet evidence-request or enforcement deadlines.

## Families child-directed ads SDK branch

For the program's overall status, applicability, provider duties, and all direct links from article 9900633, see the [Families Self-Certified Ads SDK Program topic](../families-self-certified-ads-program/families-self-certified-ads-program.en-US.md). This page retains the vendor, Maven-coordinate, version, and descendant audit rather than duplicating the changing list.

As of **October 23, 2026**, the Families Self-Certified Ads SDK Program is not accepting new applicants; current participants remain subject to existing program requirements. A third-party ads SDK must be on the current certified list/version only when it serves ads to children. Mixed-audience apps may use a non-certified SDK only for users who are not children, for example by using neutral age screening. The list and exact certified versions change, so app developers should check the [live SDK/version list](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12955712?hl=en) before every release/update. Google does not guarantee the accuracy of SDK-provided self-certification information, and certification does not transfer app-developer responsibility.

### Watermarks and self-certification test apps

The full [Watermark and test app requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9536665?hl=en) page is now open. The article says certification does not apply when a provider uses only an in-house ads solution. An SDK applying for Families self-certification must identify creatives from its inventory with a recognizable visual mark (for example, text, an image, third-party icon, URL, or redirect page). The mark must appear on **every creative** in that inventory so reviewers can verify its source. A test app must use the same inventory as the live app or a mirror of it.

The test app must be a fully built, executable Android APK for a phone form factor; use the latest released or soon-to-be-released policy-compliant SDK version; exercise the SDK, including retrieving and displaying ads; and have access to all live/serving inventory on the network without geolocation restrictions. For mixed-audience inventory, it must distinguish full inventory from inventory appropriate for children/all ages. It may not restrict testing to selected ads unless a valid neutral age screen controls that selection.

For child-directed ads, check at least the following: creatives are age appropriate and rated for age groups such as Everyone/Mature; real-time-bidding creatives are reviewed and privacy indicators reach bidders; ads are clearly distinguishable and do not induce accidental clicks, disrupt normal use, or emotionally manipulate children. Ads that interrupt normal app/game use must be dismissible after five seconds. Child-directed requests must disable personalized/interest-based ads and remarketing, and must not transmit the listed identifiers such as AAID, SIM Serial, Build Serial, BSSID, MAC, SSID, IMEI, or IMSI. A mediation platform must either use Families self-certified SDKs or provide safeguards that ensure every served ad meets the same requirements, while passing child-directed treatment and content-rating signals. Disclose SDK access, collection, use, and sharing consistently in privacy disclosures and Data safety.

The policy allows certain in-house cross-promotion of owned media and direct advertiser deals that use an SDK only for inventory management without third-party SDK certification; the app developer still remains responsible for age-appropriate ads, compliant data practices under User Data/Families, and local law. See the [Families ads SDK requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12918983?hl=en) and [program overview](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900633?hl=en). SDK providers also submit a test APK, terms/privacy/integration materials, creative-review process, and a visual identification method such as watermarking.
