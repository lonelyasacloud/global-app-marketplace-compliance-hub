## The nine policy areas

1. **Sexual content and profanity.** Apps may not contain or promote pornography, sexually gratifying content or services, compensated solicitation of sex, sexually predatory behavior, or non-consensual sexual content. Nudity may be allowed only when the primary purpose is educational, documentary, scientific, or artistic (EDSA), with contextual justification and no gratuitous presentation. A narrow exception for book/video catalog apps requires sexual titles to be a minor share of the catalog, not actively promoted (history-based recommendations and general price promotions may still show them), excludes child-endangerment, pornography, and content illegal under applicable law, and restricts minors' access. Examples include sexual nudity/acts, sexual aids or guides, illegal themes, bestiality, explicit listing terms, escort/sugar-dating services, creepshots, hidden cameras, non-consensual deepfake pornography, and sexual assault content. The article separately says that content violating this policy but considered appropriate in a particular region may be available only in that region, not elsewhere; this is a territorial limit, not a global exemption.
2. **Hate speech.** Apps may not incite hatred or promote violence based on race/ethnicity, religion, disability, age, nationality, veteran status, sexual orientation, gender, gender identity, caste, immigration status, or another characteristic associated with systemic discrimination or marginalization. Dehumanizing or inferior-group claims, hateful slurs/stereotypes, depicting a protected group as a threat, encouraging discrimination, and promoting hate-group symbols are high-risk. EDSA content related to Nazism may still be blocked in some countries under local law.
3. **Violence.** Gratuitous violence and dangerous activity may not be depicted or facilitated. Fictional game violence such as cartoons, hunting, or fishing is generally allowed. Graphic realistic violence or threats against people/animals, and content encouraging self-harm, suicide, eating disorders, choking games, or acts likely to cause serious injury or death are examples of violations.
4. **Violent extremism.** Terrorist organizations and other dangerous organizations/movements that engage in, prepare for, or claim responsibility for violence against civilians may not publish apps on Play for any purpose, including recruitment. Apps may not promote, incite, plan, prepare, or glorify such violence. EDSA material about violent extremism needs relevant educational/documentary/scientific/artistic context; this does not create a general exception for apps published by dangerous organizations.
5. **Sensitive events.** Apps may not capitalize on or treat insensitively events with major social, cultural, or political impact, including civil emergencies, disasters, public-health emergencies, conflicts, deaths, and tragedies. Examples include denying a documented major tragedy or appearing to profit without a discernible benefit to victims. EDSA content, alerts, or awareness material is generally allowed.
6. **Bullying and harassment.** Apps may not contain or facilitate threats, harassment, bullying, or exploitation. Examples include bullying conflict victims, extortion/blackmail, posting to publicly humiliate someone, and harassing tragedy victims or their friends and families.
7. **Dangerous products.** Apps may not facilitate sales of explosives, firearms, ammunition, or restricted firearm accessories. Restricted accessories include bump stocks, gatling triggers, drop-in auto sears, conversion kits, and magazines/belts holding more than 30 rounds. Apps may not provide instructions for manufacturing weapons/restricted accessories or converting firearms to automatic or simulated-automatic fire.
8. **Marijuana.** Apps may not facilitate sales of marijuana or marijuana products regardless of local legality. This includes in-app cart orders, arranging delivery or pickup, and facilitating sales of products containing THC, including CBD oil with THC.
9. **Tobacco and alcohol.** Apps may not facilitate sales of tobacco or nicotine products (including e-cigarettes, vape pens, and nicotine pouches), or encourage illegal/inappropriate alcohol, tobacco, or nicotine use. Do not depict/encourage minors' use or sales to minors, claim tobacco improves social/sexual/professional/intellectual/athletic standing, portray excessive/binge/competitive drinking favorably, or advertise/promote/feature tobacco via ads, banners, categories, or links to sellers. The article describes narrow possible allowances: limited tobacco sales in food/grocery delivery apps in certain regions with age safeguards such as ID checks on delivery, and age-gated products marketed as nicotine-cessation aids.

## Review checklist

- Review each target market, UGC surface, store asset, ad, and in-app display; the installed app's primary flow is not the only review surface.
- EDSA, regional availability, and product-specific allowances are conditional, not default permission. For the article's region-limited case, record the affected markets and availability; do not generalize local availability into a global exception. Local legality does not by itself mean Play approval.
- For sensitive events/extremism, retain the content's purpose and context. For sales of controlled goods, inspect carts, appointments, delivery/pickup flows, store links, and promotions for actual facilitation.
- Check the title, description, images, video, ads, and notifications for explicit terms, incitement, or promotion of restricted goods.
