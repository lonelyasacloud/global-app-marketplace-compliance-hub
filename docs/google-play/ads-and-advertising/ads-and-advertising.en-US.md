## Ad experience requirements

- **Content and age rating:** Ads and promoted offers, including other apps, must fit the host app's content rating. A child-directed or mixed-audience app must also follow Families ad and monetization rules.
- **Clearly identifiable ads:** Ads may not impersonate app UI, system notifications, warnings, or functional controls. Users should be able to tell which app serves an ad.
- **No disruptive ads:** Do not force an ad click or require personal information for advertising before the app can be used. Ads must stay within the app serving them and must not interfere with other apps, device functions, system controls, or exit flows. Ads that interrupt normal use must be readily dismissible; false close buttons and layouts that induce accidental taps are prohibited.
- **Interstitial timing and duration:** Unexpected full-screen interstitials are prohibited, including during the start of a game level or content segment and full-screen video before a splash/loading screen. A non-opt-in, interruptive full-screen interstitial that cannot be closed after 15 seconds is prohibited. The policy describes exceptions for explicitly opted-in/rewarded ads and interstitials that do not interrupt the user's action.
- **Lock-screen monetization:** Apps whose exclusive purpose is not the lock screen may not introduce ads or monetization on the locked display.
- **Ad fraud:** Prohibited patterns include hidden ads, automatic or non-human clicks, fake install attribution, ads outside the app, and false representation of package name, device, or inventory to ad networks.
- **Location and identifiers:** Never request location solely for ads or analytics. If permitted location data is used for ads, disclose this clearly in the privacy policy and link relevant ad-network privacy rules. AAID is only for advertising and user analytics; check the opt-out setting on every access. Analytics must not associate it with PII or persistent identifiers; advertising must not associate it with persistent identifiers, and linking it to PII for advertising requires explicit consent. Do not bridge resets to old IDs/data without explicit consent. After opt-out, profiling/personalized targeting is prohibited, while contextual ads, frequency capping, conversion tracking, reporting, and security/fraud detection remain allowed. See the [AAID topic](../advertising-id/advertising-id.en-US.md).

### Better Ads Experiences: when interstitials feel expected

The [Better Ads Experiences explainer](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12271244?hl=en) describes an unexpected full-screen interstitial as one that interrupts the user while doing something else, or appears after a button that should begin a journey. The beginning of a level/content segment is generally not a natural break; the end of a level, chapter, or content segment is more appropriate. If the product has no obvious natural break, developers should use UX judgment; integrated or non-full-screen formats are generally less disruptive.

An explicitly chosen rewarded ad is outside this policy's unexpectedness principle, but it must still comply with all other Developer Program Policies and the Ads policy. A static interstitial may appear after the app has finished loading; if there is no splash/loading screen, wait for the home screen to load. Developers remain responsible for third-party ad SDK/library behavior. The explainer is informed by the [Coalition for Better Ads](https://www.betterads.org/) research, but distinguish Play's enforceable [Ads policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753?hl=en) from external [Better Ads Standards](https://www.betterads.org/standards/): industry standards help explain user experience and do not replace Play policy text.

## “Contains ads” store label

Answer the Play Console declaration accurately. Ad SDKs, banners, interstitials, native/sponsored content, and house ads generally count; the resulting label is shown to Play users. The page allows a “More Apps/More Games” menu section to be treated differently only when it is separate from and does not interfere with gameplay or mislead users by embedding itself in play. Google may verify the declaration and display the label; misrepresentation may lead to suspension. This label does not replace Data safety, billing, sponsorship, or other declarations.

### Ad Fraud and invalid traffic: different enforcement scopes

Google Play's [Ad Fraud policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9969955?hl=en) prohibits interactions that trick an ad network into treating traffic as genuine user interest, including hidden ads, automatic clicks, fake install attribution, out-of-app pop-ups, and false app identity. Its linked AdMob [Invalid traffic guidance](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/3342054?hl=en) also covers accidental or repeated clicks, encouraging clicks, publishers clicking their own live ads, and bot traffic. Publishers remain responsible for traffic quality even when a third party generated it without their knowledge.

AdMob is a separate publisher-monetization/account system: invalid traffic can limit ad serving, suspend or disable an account, and adjust finalized month-end earnings. These account actions are not the same as Google Play app removal or suspension. Prevention practices, Android/iOS test ads, placement guides, account appeals, and earnings details are summarized in the [Mobile Unwanted Software and ad-fraud topic](../mobile-unwanted-software/mobile-unwanted-software.en-US.md). Google's prevention overview describes automated filtering and human review but does not disclose account-specific detection details.

## Better Ads Standards descendants: examples, research, dates, and governance

### Policy layers

Google Play's formal [Ads policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753?hl=en) governs app distribution. The [Better Ads Experiences explainer](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12271244?hl=en) explains unexpected full-screen interstitials with natural-break, rewarded-ad, and post-load static-interstitial examples. The Coalition for Better Ads (CBA) [Better Ads Standards](https://www.betterads.org/standards/) are external industry standards based on consumer research. Play says its experience guidance is “inspired by” CBA; that does not incorporate every CBA web/video classification wholesale into Play. A format not listed in a CBA standard for a particular environment is not thereby compliant with Play. Likewise, CBA web ad-density thresholds are not native Android app ad-density limits under Play policy.

The Play Ads body has separate enforceable requirements: ads and offers are part of the app; ads cannot impersonate app/system UI, force clicks, or interfere with device controls; ads that disrupt normal use must be easy to dismiss; unexpected full-screen interstitials cannot appear at the beginning of a level/content segment, and full-screen video cannot precede the splash/loading screen; a non-opt-in, interruptive full-screen interstitial that cannot be closed after 15 seconds is disallowed. Explicitly chosen rewarded ads and non-interrupting placements receive only the scope of the exceptions stated in the formal text; they are not blanket exemptions.
