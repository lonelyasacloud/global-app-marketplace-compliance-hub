## Scope and current rollout

Android's official guidance says developers must register app package names so their apps can be installed on protected, certified Android devices. Device enforcement began **September 30, 2026** for Android 7+ certified devices in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore, and Thailand, covering participating app stores including Google Play, Honor, OPlus/OPPO, Samsung Galaxy Store, Transsion/Palm Store, vivo, and Xiaomi. Google plans to expand protection to certified devices globally and more third-party stores in **2027 and later**.

This staged device-side enforcement does not create a Play distribution exemption. Google's [Play Console Requirements](../play-console-requirements/play-console-requirements.en-US.md) separately require package registration for Play apps developers want to keep distributing. Google says 99% of Play apps were auto-registered, but developers should still check the Console homepage and each app's status and address packages that remain unregistered. Off-Play distribution uses the corresponding registration path.

## Package registration and signing keys

### Distribution only outside Google Play

Create and verify an Android Developer Console account. Package registration generally requires the unique package name and SHA-256 certificate fingerprint for the app signing key. For a package already used by another developer, the claimant may need to sign an APK with their private key and upload it with a verification code supplied by the Console in the APK resources. If the current key does not qualify, developers may request shared/exception access with a reason and supporting evidence.

Duplicate-package priority uses known install counts: a key with more than 50% of known installs receives priority; if no key has a majority, keys with at least 50 installs are eligible; if no key reaches that cohort size, registration is first come, first served for developers with known keys. Losing the signing private key may prevent registration; Google recommends secure key management. A package may have multiple verified signing keys.

### Distribution through Google Play

Use Play Console to manage apps distributed both on and off Play. Eligible apps using Play App Signing can be identified/registered automatically; creating a new Play app automatically associates its package name with the account. If another developer already uses the name, Console prompts you to choose another. A package already used outside Play can continue to be used on Play. Check per-app status on the Android developer verification page in Console; Android Studio Panda 4+ also displays registration status.

### Managed Google Play private apps: keep the two exemptions separate

Starting **September 30, 2026**, a private app deployed to an unmanaged Play Protect certified Android device must be associated with a verified developer identity and have its package registered to remain installable and updatable. “Managed” means enrolled in Android Enterprise through an EMM/MDM, including fully managed devices and work profiles; personal or unenrolled devices are unmanaged. Private apps distributed only to managed devices or work profiles do not need this device-side registration.

Choose the path based on distribution and device management:

1. **Play Console:** The main management platform for apps distributed through Google Play. Developers can verify identity there; using Play Console removes the need to register the same package separately through Android Developer Console. The FAQ also says a Play Console account can centralize verification for apps distributed both on and off Play.
2. **Android Developer Console:** For developers distributing outside Google Play to verify identity and register package names; it is not an app publishing platform. Private apps that need unmanaged-device coverage can use this path if identity verification has not been completed in Play Console. The current Help page requires a Full Distribution Organization account, corporate-domain email, Google Payments Profile matching the organization’s legal name/address/D-U-N-S, verified contact email, and a US$25 fee. Existing packages require a SHA-256 signing-certificate fingerprint and a cryptographic challenge using the production private key: place the nonce in a dummy APK's assets, sign it, and upload it to prove key ownership. The Help page says it will provide more steps for Play-managed signing keys.
3. **Enroll devices in Android Enterprise management:** Once devices are enrolled through EMM/MDM, private apps are exempt from device-side enforcement, including on fully managed devices and work profiles.
4. **End-user advanced flow:** Users can enable the advanced flow in Android developer options to manually allow an unregistered private app on an unmanaged device. This is a user-accepted install path, not developer identity verification or Play policy approval.

Do not extend the Play target API exception for apps that are permanently private and restricted to a specific organization into an exemption from device-side developer verification. Identity registration, Play publishing/policy review, and device management are separate checks. Before release, confirm the actual EMM enrollment of target devices and package/account status in the relevant Console. See [Android Enterprise Help: developer verification for private apps](https://support.google.com/work/android/answer/17266330?hl=en), [Android Enterprise terminology](https://developers.google.com/android/work/terminology), and the [Play private-app publishing topic](../play-console-requirements/play-console-requirements.en-US.md).

## Open-source distribution, Play manual registration, and automation best practices

### Open-source app platforms

Android's [open-source app registration guide](https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/open-source-app-registration) says registration is not required to keep distributing an app on an open-source platform. However, users of unregistered apps must complete the device advanced flow; registration preserves the familiar installation experience. A store integrated with the Android Developer Console API may handle package and key registration, but the developer still needs a verified Android Developer Console or Play Console identity and must link the app to that identity.

- **New app, platform uses the developer's key:** register the package name and add that key's SHA-256 certificate fingerprint.
- **Platform re-signs the app:** add the platform signing certificate fingerprint as another key. If the app reaches 50 or more Android installs before the platform key is registered, ownership proof for an existing package may be required.
- **Existing app re-signed by a platform:** download the final platform-signed APK, select an eligible certificate, put the Console-generated verification snippet in `assets/adi-registration.properties`, publish a version containing the file, then download and upload the final APK from the platform to prove key ownership.
- **The general Play Console registration guide** likewise requires an APK signed by the matching private key for an existing package. If the key is delegated to a third-party store, upload that store's final signed APK rather than a local pre-re-sign build.

### API integration best practices

The [Android Developer Console API guide](https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/developer-console-api) is for third-party stores and individual developers. It supports programmatic package registration, key management, and proof of key ownership. Calls require the OAuth 2.0 Web Server flow and the `androiddeveloperconsole` scope. Because account data belongs to the Google Account, the guide says API keys, service accounts, and Workload Identity Federation cannot replace user authorization.

Before launching OAuth consent, an integration should explain why account access is needed; list authorized accounts and clearly show account names/verification state, package registration state, and key fingerprints/status. Unverified accounts should be directed to complete verification first. Branding guidance requires the “Continue with Google” or “Sign in with Google” button label, the full product name Android Developer Console, the term “package name,” and the phrase “register a package name”; do not abbreviate the product as ADC or say “claim a package.” These are API-integration UI recommendations and branding requirements, not a policy that every developer must use the API.

Do not conflate the two APIs: the read-only [Android Developer ID Status API](https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/check-registration-status) checks whether a package name is registered and whether a package-name/SHA-256 fingerprint pair matches a registered key. It uses a Google Cloud API key, with a default quota of 1,000 requests per project per day. States distinguish `REGISTERED`, `NOT_REGISTERED`, and `REGISTERED_WITH_ANOTHER_CERTIFICATE_FINGERPRINT`. This API only checks status; it cannot register an app. The state-changing Android Developer Console API requires Google Account OAuth authorization.

#### Android Developer Console API registration method flow

The Android API integration guide embeds REST method names in its workflow rather than providing a complete request/response reference:

| Stage | API methods named by the guide | Purpose and branch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Select an account | `ListDeveloperAccounts` | Lists developer accounts covered by the current OAuth grant; the UI should show `displayName` and `verificationState`. An account marked `NOT_VERIFIED` must complete identity verification before registration. |
| Read packages and keys | `ListAndroidPackages`, `ListAndroidPackageKeys` | Show package names, registration states, SHA-256 fingerprints, and key states so the user can select the correct package and signing key. |
| Determine the registration path | `CreateAndroidPackage`, `GetAndroidPackageRegistrationPolicy` | A new package name can be registered with its signing public key. For an existing package, retrieve known keys and `keySelectionStrategy`. `SELECT_KEY_FROM_LIST` requires selecting from `knownKeys` and proving key ownership; `USE_ANY_KEY` accepts a supplied key without that ownership proof. |
| Add a key | `CreateAndroidPackageKey` | Associates the selected or newly supplied public key with the package name. Creating the key record does not by itself mean registration/review is complete. |
| Prove key ownership | `VerifyAndroidPackageKeyOwnership` | In flows that require proof, retrieve a `verificationToken`, place it in `assets/adi-registration.properties`, sign the APK with the matching private key, and submit it for verification. If a store manages the key, the integration must automate proof as described and tell the developer. |
| Submit a business rationale | `JustifyAndroidPackageKeyRegistration` | Submit a detailed rationale only when a known key has `justificationRequired` set to `REQUIRED`. The official page says review can take up to 24 hours. |

**Status-field boundary:** The same guide's glossary lists key states as `DRAFT`, `OWNERSHIP_VERIFIED`, `IN_REVIEW`, `REGISTERED`, and `PENDING_TRANSFER`, while its UI guidance lists `REGISTERED_ACTIVE`. This source-page label difference is preserved. This review did not obtain a readable machine-readable API schema, so it does not merge the values or infer the complete state machine, request bodies, error codes, or retry behavior. Integrations should handle states according to current API responses and available reference documentation.

This maps the API workflow and interface guidance; it does not require every Play developer to use the API. Play package-registration obligations, identity verification, and Console actions remain governed by the relevant policy and account state.

### Manual Play Console registration and troubleshooting

The Play Console [package registration guide](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16984799) says **all Play package names must be registered from September 30, 2026**; that date has passed as of this review. Console auto-registers eligible apps, but developers should check the homepage and each app's Android developer verification page. For apps not auto-registered, follow the prompts to select an eligible fingerprint, add the verification snippet to `assets/adi-registration.properties`, sign and upload a proof APK; the Help page says this is not submission of the actual app APK. If a fingerprint is ineligible, a business-rationale request is required and can be rejected. Apply only when there is a legitimate reason to share the package name; otherwise consider using a new package name. Additional keys usually take a few minutes to propagate.

## Play Console developer identity verification

[Verify your developer identity information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10841920?hl=en) treats identity, contact, website/device, and package-name checks as separate steps. The article says developers can begin verification 60 days before their individual deadline; the actual due date still comes from the account's Play Console tasks. If identity or contact details cannot be verified, the developer presence and apps may be removed, and the app cannot be republished until verification is complete.

Identity documents and public details depend on account type. Personal accounts use the legal name/address in the linked Payments Profile and require a government-issued identity document. Organization accounts also require official organization documents and a D-U-N-S number, except that known government organizations may qualify for an exception. Organization name/address should match the D&B record; a large business may have several D-U-N-S numbers, so select the number for the legal entity represented by the developer account. D-U-N-S is free through Dun & Bradstreet, and the Help article says issuance can take up to 30 days. Organizations in unsupported regions that cannot get a D-U-N-S number should contact support before creating the account; a general lack of a number is not an automatic exemption.

**Verification order and best practices:** every developer verifies the public developer email with a six-digit code; organizations also verify the public developer phone, while personal-account phone requirements can depend on regional rules. Google-only contact email and phone are also verified but are not shown to users. Before phone verification, an organization must complete identity and website checks; a personal account must complete identity and device checks. Organizations should use durable, organization-owned email addresses rather than generic or personal addresses; multi-user teams may share notifications. Public developer email/phone must remain reachable, or apps may be removed. Changing an email or phone generally requires reverification. For new personal accounts, the article describes device verification with the owner account in the Play Console mobile app on a physical, non-rooted Android 10+ device. This account-onboarding check is separate from Android developer package registration and from end-user installation verification.

Organization website verification usually associates the site and developer account through Search Console. If both use the same Google Account, approval can be automatic; otherwise the site's registered owner must approve. A social-media profile cannot substitute for a verifiable website. Identity documents must exactly match the legal name, organization, and address in the linked Payments Profile. Photo IDs must be valid, clear, and in color, not photocopies; all seventeen reviewed English regional views require proof of address with an exact name/address match and an issue date within 90 days. EEA/UK identity documents must not have fields obscured. The country selector changes the document list, so this is a snapshot of seventeen reviewed English regions, plus a Simplified Chinese China view, not a global list.

### Merchant payout bank: verification, thresholds, and regional differences

The [merchant bank-account article](https://support.google.com/paymentscenter/answer/7161440) requires an account that can receive EFT or wire transfers and is generally in the same country/region as the merchant payments account; EFT uses the local currency and wire transfers use USD. EEA merchants can ask support to nominate a SEPA-reachable account in any EEA country. The account-holder name must match the bank's records. Google says it will never request bank-account details by email. The article contains bank-field and formatting tables by bank location; use its current regional selector because those fields can change. This KB does not copy the full dynamic country table.

The [bank-verification article](https://support.google.com/paymentscenter/answer/7161378) distinguishes payment paths: bank verification applies to EFT countries; US EFT merchants may verify instantly, while other applicable locations typically verify a small Google test deposit (under US$1, processed in up to about three business days) through the payments profile. Wire payouts do not receive a test deposit. After verification, set the intended payout account as primary. Minimum payout amounts vary by country and currency. Its troubleshooting link currently lands on the [Google Payments Center Help directory](https://support.google.com/paymentscenter), not a specific error article; Play developer payment issues route to the [Play Console support article](https://support.google.com/paymentscenter/answer/16144127).

Keep the actual payout minimum separate from a configurable threshold. The [wire-transfer article](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/2700656) states a US$100 minimum balance for wire payouts, paid in USD; bank fees can range from US$0 to US$50 or more. Argentine merchants may need local supporting documents for their bank. [Order processing and payouts](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/137997) says the previous month's orders are generally paid around the 15th of the following month, with additional bank posting time; it also says buyers have two hours to cancel an app/game purchase before Google automatically charges the card. Local-currency payouts and wire transfers have different minimums. The [threshold settings article](https://support.google.com/paymentscenter/answer/7163445) lists country/currency-dependent thresholds and says developers can self-hold payouts for up to one year; changes made by the 21st can affect the current month's cycle. These are merchant payout/order operations, not new Developer Program Policy obligations. Verify actual eligibility, account state, and timing in the current payments profile and with the bank.

## Maintaining verified Play developer-account information

[View and manage account information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13634081) applies only after an account has completed verification under Play Console Requirements. An unverified account should start with [the account-creation requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13628312). Only the account owner can change developer-account information. Google requires accurate, current details; stale identity or contact data can restrict the account and lead to app removal.

Keep these four field groups separate during review:

- **Store-listing contact details:** an email address is required to publish or update apps, and the contact details entered here appear on the app's Play listing.
- **Private Google contacts:** contact name, email, phone, and email language are for Google only. Email and phone require one-time-password verification. An organization's contact email should represent it, use its website domain, differ from the Google Account email, and may be a group mailing list.
- **Public developer contacts:** the developer email and, where applicable, phone number are verified before users see them. A developer-name change appears after Google review and approval. Korea-based developers must also provide a business registration number, e-commerce license number, and licensing agency; these fields appear to users in Korea.
- **Legal identity and merchant address:** organization name, address, and D-U-N-S come from the linked Google Payments profile. Merchant accounts that monetize through paid apps or in-app purchases must show the full address from that profile on Google Play. A same-country name/address change can generally be made in the existing profile; a country change or different organization D-U-N-S requires a new verified payments profile.

**Notifications and account lifecycle:** Policy and Payments and tax emails cannot be turned off. Turning off optional tips does not suppress mandatory service announcements, which still go to the developer-profile email. Use the [app-transfer checklist](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247) to move published or unpublished apps. To close an account with published apps, back up its data, transfer the apps, then reply to the transfer confirmation. Deleting a Google Account does not automatically delete a Play developer account, and developer names must be unique across accounts.

## Payments-profile identity changes and account-type conversion

[Update developer identity details managed by a Google payments profile](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16260648?hl=en) says only the account owner can start identity-profile changes from **Developer account → About you** in Play Console. Keep the identity-verification payments profile distinct from the merchant payments profile used to receive earnings; some developers use one profile for both, while others do not.

- **Name or address within the same country:** update the existing linked payments profile. Google may request identity or organization documents.
- **Country or organization D-U-N-S change:** create or select a new payments profile in Console, provide and verify the required details, then have the owner link it to the developer account and confirm. D-U-N-S registration is free; the Help article warns about paid intermediaries. Organizations generally need a D-U-N-S number. A known government organization/agency may qualify to verify without one; if Console does not offer that path and the organization cannot provide a number, use in-Console support or the public form when Console access is unavailable.
- **Personal-to-Organization conversion:** the Personal account must be fully verified with no pending verification warnings. Verify the official organization website first, then select/create the Organization payments profile and provide organization type, size, phone, and separately verified Google-only and public contact details. Complete any identity review, link the verified profile, and confirm the conversion. Wait at least **72 hours** after conversion before submitting new apps so account details can sync and reduce redundant rejection risk.
- **Organization-to-Personal conversion:** the account type cannot be changed in that direction. Create and verify a new Personal developer account, then transfer apps using the [app-transfer checklist](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247).

## Release and migration checklist

1. List distribution channels and target countries/devices separately: Google Play, other stores, website/enterprise, testing, and personal sharing.
2. Check package registration, signing certificates, and key ownership per app in Play Console and the applicable Android Developer Console. Do not rely only on the “99% auto-registered” aggregate.
3. Prepare the account path, identity/address evidence, D-U-N-S, site verification, and fees early; organization documents may take time.
4. For duplicate package names, prepare signing-key/install evidence and protect private signing keys.
5. For Limited Distribution, current English and Chinese guides and Android Developer Console Help all state a maximum of 20 devices; check the specific account’s Console limit before planning beyond it. Record that ADB testing differs from the consumer sideload flow.
6. For Managed Google Play private apps, identify whether target devices are fully managed/work-profile or unmanaged certified devices, then use the corresponding package-registration path.
7. Track the 2027 global expansion and recheck regional enforcement, participating stores, and Console fields against the live source.

This is a bilingual paraphrase, not a full official-page mirror or proof of account compliance.
