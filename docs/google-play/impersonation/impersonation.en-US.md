**Reviewed October 22, 2026.** Google Play prohibits apps and developer accounts that impersonate others or misrepresent identity, ownership, or primary purpose. Coordination with others to mislead users is also prohibited. The developer name, app title, icon, description, interface, and store assets should accurately show who publishes the app, whether it is related to another brand/entity/app, and what it does.

The separate [Misrepresentation policy topic](../misrepresentation/misrepresentation.en-US.md) overlaps with impersonation and explicitly discusses coordinated deception, false country of origin, and concealing identity around matters of public concern.

## Common risks and release review

- A developer name falsely implies an official relationship with Google, a company, institution, or organization.
- A national emblem, brand mark, or name/icon similar to another app or service makes users believe the app is official or affiliated.
- An app claims to be an established person/entity's “Official” app without necessary authorization, or places another party's mark in its title/icon to imply endorsement, authorization, or affiliation.
- App descriptions, in-app elements, or coordinated developer activity conceal identity, ownership, or the app's primary purpose.

Before release, compare developer identity details, app title/icon/description, first-run and login screens, brand/government marks, domain, support channels, and localized assets in every market. For a legitimate affiliation, retain evidence showing the authorization scope, market, and term. A similar name or an assumption that users can tell the difference does not guarantee compliance.

## Impersonation vs. copyright/trademark reporting

The Play Impersonation policy addresses whether a store listing misleads users into believing an app is connected to another entity or app. A user can report that deception through the Play policy process without asserting intellectual-property rights. A copyright, trademark, or other rights-holder claim belongs in the corresponding legal process; a wrong form may delay or prevent processing. These routes do not replace each other or decide ownership rights. See the [Intellectual Property topic](../intellectual-property/intellectual-property.en-US.md) for asset licensing, copyright/trademark complaints, and Advance Notice.
