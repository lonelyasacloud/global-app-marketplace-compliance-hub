## Summary

The agreement governs Play distribution. Developers need authority to accept for an entity, accurate Console details, and compliance with the agreement, law, and Play policies. Paid distribution requires an approved Payments Profile; fees, taxes, and refunds depend on territory and related terms. Initially free products must remain free.

Developers are responsible for product/permission disclosures, support, privacy, tax settings, and distribution rights. For paid products or in-app transactions, respond within 3 business days, or within 24 hours for Google-marked urgent issues. Developers retain IP but license Google for Play/Android operations, hosting, promotion, improvement, and compliance checks; users also receive product-use rights.

Removal may leave user rights and support duties in place; legal takedowns may require refunds. Google may reject, remove, suspend, limit, or prohibit products/accounts and withhold payments. The agreement covers changes, termination, warranties, liability, indemnity, export/sanctions, and California law.

### Comparing the February 5, 2024 archive with the September 15, 2025 current agreement

On October 23, 2026, directly compared the [2024 English archived text](https://play.google/developer-distribution-agreement/archive.html) with the [2025 current English text](https://play.google/developer-distribution-agreement.html), then checked Google's [Summary of Changes](https://play.google/developer-distribution-agreement/summary.html). The table expands only the 2025 clauses Google explicitly lists; it is not a full word-for-word redline or legal advice. DDA §16.1 says the English text controls other language versions.

| Clause | 2024 archive → 2025 current text | Practical reading and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| §1 definition of Taxes | The 2024 version excluded communication taxes and similar charges and included Transaction Taxes. The 2025 version includes communication and Withholding Taxes, while excluding charges based on import/export of goods (including duties/tariffs). | This updates the contract definition's scope. Responsibility for reporting/payment still depends on §§3.5–3.6, the applicable territory, and payment setup; the definition alone does not determine who handles a particular tax. |
| §§2.1 and 3.1 country/territory links | Google says the country-list links were updated. Clicking the links in both versions on October 23, 2026 led to the same current [Supported locations for distribution to Google Play users](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10532353) Help article. | The Help article and its downstream country table can change; the archived agreement does not freeze their historical contents. This confirms the current link destination, not the country list as it existed in 2024. |
| §3.3 tax settings | The 2024 version authorized Google to charge, collect, and remit relevant taxes as required by law. The 2025 version says according to developer settings or as required by law. Google's summary says the wording anticipates global tax categories. | The settings route is more explicit, but the same clause leaves developers responsible for relevant taxes, fees, and surcharges and for accurate product tax classification; classification changes apply only to future sales. Do not read Console calculation as transferring the contractual responsibility. |
| §3.6 Withholding Taxes | The 2024 version already covered lawful withholding, cross-border payments/telecom taxes, timely tax documentation, and 90 days' written notice before a non-U.S./Singapore tax resident changes service location. The 2025 version adds that Google may apply a reduced rate/exemption when it determines one is available, after the developer provides (and when needed updates) requested documents and represents eligibility; relief applies only to future sales, and W-9/W-8 forms may be requested. | A reduction is not automatic or guaranteed: Google determines availability and the documentation must be completed. The 90-day service-location notice was already in the 2024 text; it is not a new 2025 requirement. Check eligibility and documents against the developer's tax/payment account. |
| §5.3 user license | The 2024 text granted users a nonexclusive, worldwide, perpetual license to use, display, and perform the Product. The 2025 text expressly adds modifying the color of or adding themes to Product icons, and displaying the modified icons/Product. | This expressly licenses user color/theme adjustments; it does not require the developer to build a theme feature or transfer the Product's source code or other IP to users. |

### Comparing the October 3, 2022 archive with the August 29, 2023 version

On October 24, 2026, reopened the official English [2022 archived agreement](https://play.google/developer-distribution-agreement/archive-v3.html) and [2023 archived agreement](https://play.google/developer-distribution-agreement/archive-v2.html), then compared the clauses named in Google's [Summary of Changes](https://play.google/developer-distribution-agreement/summary.html). This is a comparison of the publisher-announced changes, not a word-for-word redline of the entire agreement.

| Clause | 2022 text → 2023 text | Practical reading and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| §3.1 Play relationship | The 2023 text adds that Google permits use of Play for the Product subject to the DDA's terms. The 2022 §2.1 already said developers must accept the Agreement to use Play. | Treat this as a clearer link between Google's permission and the Agreement, not the first appearance of an acceptance condition. |
| §3.5 transaction taxes | Google's summary says the 2023 revision removes the “from time to time” and “sole discretion” wording from this clause. | The change narrows those expressions; it does not remove the developer's listed tax responsibilities or make the referenced country/condition rules static. Check the operative agreement and current tax Help for today's terms. |
| §4.9 user information | The 2022 clause expressly barred using user information obtained through Play to sell or distribute Products outside Play; that sentence is absent from the 2023 version. | Deleting this DDA sentence is not permission for unrestricted off-Play use. | §§5.1 and 6.5 product/license scope | The 2023 wording ties Google's license to making the Product available on Play, limits security-partner sublicensing to compliance review, and says the relevant authorization ends when the DDA terminates. | §7.1 promotions | The 2023 version says that where applicable law prohibits it, Google will not condition or withhold promotions, featuring, or marketing based on whether the developer releases on Play first. | This is expressly limited to what applicable law prohibits; it is not a general promise of equal promotion in every territory or program. |
| §8.3 enforcement and appeals | The 2023 text removes “in its sole discretion” from the awareness/determination sentence and adds a link to the appeals process. The listed rejection, removal, suspension, visibility-limiting, and reclassification powers remain. | A linked appeal route does not suspend or erase the stated enforcement powers; see [Managing Policy Violations and Appeals](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899142) for the separate operational process. |
| §10.2 developer termination | The 2023 text expressly says the developer may terminate at any time, then requires unpublishing all Products and ceasing Console/credential use. | The right to terminate does not waive obligations for previously distributed Products or payment/refund terms. |
| §12 warranty disclaimer | The 2023 text extends “to the maximum extent permitted by law” language across the disclaimer clauses; the 2022 text already used that qualifier in §12.1 but not in §§12.2–12.3. | This records contract wording, not a conclusion about enforceability in a particular jurisdiction. |
