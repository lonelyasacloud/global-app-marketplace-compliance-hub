## Package names, artifacts, and versions

- **Package names are permanent:** Play requires each app package name to be unique and permanent. It cannot be deleted and reused for another app. A new Play app is associated with Android developer verification; see developer verification [Android developer verification](../android-developer-verification/android-developer-verification.en-US.md).
- **AAB and generated APKs:** Google Play uses Android App Bundles to generate APKs optimized for device configurations. Developers upload one signed bundle, and Play manages distribution APKs. See the child-link table and [Play App Signing in the release workflow](../publishing/play-release-workflow.en-US.md#play-app-signing-and-automatic-protection-body-summaries-and-key-direct-descendants).
- **Artifact states:** The source article describes Draft, Active, and Archived APK states: not yet served, currently served, or previously active but no longer served. The newer Console App Bundle Explorer can inspect Details, Downloads, and Delivery by version. It is account-only; user-specific state requires sign-in.
- **[Version codes](../app-version-codes-and-apk-distribution/app-version-codes-and-apk-distribution.en-US.md):** Increment `versionCode` for every app update. The current Play Console guide sets a maximum of **2,100,000,000**; uploads above it are blocked. Android `MAXINT` and Play's upload limit are not the same. Leave room for future updates.
- **Target API:** Every uploaded bundle must meet the current target API requirements. As checked on October 22, the official page listed the August 31, 2026 submission thresholds, form-factor exceptions, visibility rules for older apps, and an extension to November 1, 2026. Recheck the [target API topic](../target-api/target-api-level.en-US.md) and current Console warning before relying on a deadline.
- **Download size:** Play measures compressed download size for app bundles, feature modules, and asset packs. The current Help article lists 500 MB for the base module and each feature module, 1.5 GB per asset pack, 4 GB cumulative for install-time modules/packs, 30 GB cumulative for fast-follow/on-demand packs, and 34 GB total per app. Apps above 200 MB trigger a non-blocking mobile-data download notice; apps above 1 GB must use minSdk API 21 or higher. Legacy APK publishing remains subject to a 100 MB limit. Recheck Google's [size limits](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859372?hl=en) because limits and packaging rules can change. Actual download-size trends appear in the App size page after a Production release.

## Store listing, languages, and support contacts

The main store listing is shared across testing tracks. Current field limits are **30** characters for app name, **80** for short description, and **4,000** for full description; the same limits apply to full-width and half-width characters. Google warns that repeated or irrelevant keyword use in the name, description, or promotional description can harm the user experience and result in suspension. See [store listing and asset best practices](../store-listing-best-practices/store-listing-best-practices.en-US.md) for content, asset, and accuracy requirements.

The default language is en-US. Developers can add translations for app information and localized screenshots/graphics; users see matching assets when their language preferences match. If developers do not provide translations, Play may show machine-translated listing text with a notice. The Help page says Armenian, Raeto-Romance, Tagalog, and Zulu are not supported for automated translation. Store/product translations and Gemini app-string translation are also described; check the [translation and localization guide](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9844778?hl=en) and current Console for language availability, account eligibility, and pricing.

Choose a category that reflects the app's primary purpose. Select at most five tags, and make their relevance clear from the listing or first-use experience. Reassess tags after significant content or functionality changes. Accessibility tags can signal screen-reader, visual, hearing, learning, motor, or communication support. See [categories and tags](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859673?hl=en) and the local [store listing topic](../store-listing-best-practices/store-listing-best-practices.en-US.md).

Every app must provide a valid, accurate support email. Google strongly recommends a contact website; a phone number is an optional additional contact method. These details appear on the store listing. The separate user-support guide also requires responses to paid-app/in-app-purchase questions within **three business days** and urgent product issues raised by Google within **24 hours**. Refunds must follow applicable law and the developer's refund policy; notify users of policy changes. An FAQ, website, or monitored forum can improve self-service. That article links to consumer [Google Play refund policies](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/2479637) and developer [Play Console order/refund management legacy entry](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859683), which redirects to the [current refund operations guide](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/2741495). These pages explain different buyer and developer flows; refund and cancellation are not interchangeable. See the [payments and subscriptions topic](../payments/payments-and-subscriptions.en-US.md).

## Related public guide: getting discovered in Google Play search

The official search index also surfaced the public Help article [Get discovered on Google Play search](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/4448378?hl=en). It is a related Help article to the “create a compelling store listing” prompt in the app-setup flow. Because the source setup page could not be opened and did not expose the anchor's href, **we cannot confirm that this is the exact destination of that unresolved source link**.

The article recommends a unique, accessible, focused title; user-centered description copy with the most important text near the top; compliance with spam, keyword-stuffing, intellectual-property, and impersonation rules; screenshots that show real supported-device experiences with unique alt text; and localization for other markets. It says Play search considers the overall app experience, user behavior, and feedback, including ratings, reviews, downloads, and other factors, while the weights and values are proprietary. Its actionable advice is to maintain a meaningful experience, improve the app regularly, invite feedback, and address user issues. It does not disclose a keyword-density formula or public ranking recipe.

### Descendants of the search guide: translation, ratings, and feedback

- [App Translation Service overview](https://support.google.com/l10n/answer/6359997) describes vetted translation vendors for store copy, app strings, and in-app products, as well as reusing existing translations. This overview gives an estimate of about USD 50 and four to five business days for a typical order. The more current Play Help article says pricing can start at USD 0.07 per word and completion is within seven days. These are different estimates, not a fixed quote or SLA; check the current Console quote and promised delivery date for the actual language pair and word count.
- [Create custom store listings](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9867158) can target countries/regions, keywords, ad traffic, pre-registration, churned/non-buying users, and custom audiences, with up to 50 listings. Custom listings are not automatically translated; contact information, privacy policy, and category remain shared across variants. Each variant must still follow metadata rules. See the [store-listing topic](../store-listing-best-practices/store-listing-best-practices.en-US.md) for its conditions and descendants.
- [Android's Localize your app](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/resources/localization) is engineering guidance, not a Play review rule. This pass sampled default-resource fallback, `res/values/strings.xml`, locale resource files, translator context, and XLIFF placeholders. Missing default resources can prevent the app from launching in an unsupported locale. The large guide's API/example descendants were not exhausted.
- Direct descendants of [ratings and reviews](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/138230?hl=en) include consumer instructions for rating/editing reviews, Console ratings/reviews workspaces, testing feedback, posting policy, reporting, and monthly exports. The consumer page only explains the user's review flow. [Report inappropriate reviews](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/7318385) says to report only content that violates the Comment Posting Policy; a specialist decides whether it is removed. [Monthly reports](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6135870) include review/rating data, while account-specific reports in Cloud Storage remain permission-gated.
- [Custom peer groups](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9842755?hl=en) allow one ratings group and one Android vitals group. A vitals group can be edited up to three times per month and requires 8–12 apps; unavailable peers are replaced with recommended apps. Opting out of Android vitals peer benchmarking does not remove an app from overall category-rating comparisons. This explains measurement scope; it is not a ratings policy or ranking formula. For testing tracks, [the testing guide](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9845334?hl=en) says tester feedback does not count toward the public rating.
- The consumer [early-access and beta guide](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/7003180?hl=en) adds the user-facing feedback rule: only the developer can see private feedback on Early Access/Beta apps, and users must both rate and write feedback to submit it. This aligns with the developer testing guide's public-rating exclusion, but is consumer help rather than a new developer obligation. [Leaving a beta program](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/15654751?hl=en) warns that leaving and uninstalling can lose progress/customizations and that a tester is not guaranteed the spot again; switching to the public version means uninstalling the beta and installing the public build. The linked [uninstall](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/13627402?hl=en) and [reinstall/re-enable](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/113410?hl=en) pages are user-operation instructions, not Store review requirements.
- The beta guide also links to the consumer [Data safety explainer](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/11416267?hl=en), which explains the public collection/sharing, security, and deletion disclosures. It says the app-permissions list and Data safety section come from different sources and may not match. This complements the local [Data safety policy topic](../data-safety/data-safety.en-US.md): the consumer page explains how to read a listing; the developer topic covers declaration duties. The page's complete data-type vocabulary and 40+ related-resource descendants were not expanded in this branch.
- [Add developer account users and manage permissions](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9844686?hl=en) lists “Reply to reviews” as a separate permission. It lets users reply and change the contact information used in suggested replies; users without it can still view ratings and reviews but cannot reply. Permissions can be scoped to the account or an app; invitations can have an expiry date, and access continues indefinitely if no date is set. This is Console access control, not a change to comment policy.
- The [Reply to Reviews API](https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/reply-to-reviews) exposes only recent feedback with comments from production versions (reviews created or modified in the last seven days); use Console for testing feedback. API replies are public, limited to 350 characters, and must not include sensitive user information. Google discourages automated replies that will be replaced manually later. Per app, the documented quotas are 200 GET requests per hour and 2,000 POST replies per day. OAuth or a service account is required; service accounts need the Reply to reviews permission. The API does not replace the full review-history CSV in Console.
- The API's [Getting Started](https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/getting_started) guide requires a Google Cloud project, enabling the Developer API, and configuring a service account or OAuth client. The current guide says linking the Play developer account to the Cloud project is no longer required. Play Console still controls account/app permissions, and service-account credentials must be stored securely on a server. Account-specific setup remains sign-in gated.
- The linked legacy Android “Reviews” page now redirects to the [Google Play Reviews business page](https://google.play/business/reviews/). It recommends timely replies, reporting policy-violating reviews, device filtering, and exports; promotional metrics are not policy commitments or ranking guarantees. ## Pre-release checklist

1. Fix the unique package name and retain bundle, upload-key, and version-code plans; review signing choice and server/third-party certificate fingerprints before the first rollout.
2. Check the build against current target API, AAB size, form factor, and testing requirements. Confirm whether the 12-tester/14-day rule applies to a new Personal account.
3. Ensure the listing accurately reflects functionality, audience, category, tags, price type, languages, and assets for supported devices. The main listing is shared across testing tracks.
4. Provide a working support email and meet response times for paid-app/IAP questions. If reviewers need to log in, provide usable credentials as described in [app review preparation](../app-review-preparation/app-review-preparation.en-US.md).
5. Treat App content, policy/export declarations, the DDA, and the Play App Signing Terms as separate items. One checkbox does not replace the others.
