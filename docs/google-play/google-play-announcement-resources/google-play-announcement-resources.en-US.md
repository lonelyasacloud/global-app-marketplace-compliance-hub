## Deduplicated key resources and best practices

### Publishing, white-label apps, and store listings

- [Best Practices for White Label Developers](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15884185) (announcement 11): Google recommends decentralized account structures to isolate client/app violations; give each app distinct, accurate branding and listing content; run quality and policy checks before submission; provide working reviewer credentials; avoid changes that alter the item under review; monitor Policy status; allow for review time; and consider Managed Publishing to control release timing after approval.
- [Helpful tips to get your app published on Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15191715) (announcements 11 and 15): Ensure actual functionality matches the title, description, and images; provide usable reviewer credentials; test installation, startup, stability, and key flows on test tracks; fix rejection causes before resubmitting.
- [Best practices for your store listing](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13393723) (announcement 11): Keep titles, descriptions, and assets concise, accurate, and original. Avoid keyword stuffing, unsupported ranking/discount/affiliation claims, and misleading screenshots. Localized text must also match real functionality.
- [Play Console Requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10788890) and [Requirements for providing sign-in details for review](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15748846) (announcements 11 and 20): Reviewers must be able to access functionality behind sign-in. Keep credentials, access instructions, regional restrictions, and test conditions valid and available.

### AI, child safety, privacy, and SDKs

- [Best Practices to Safeguard AI-Generated Content](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16353813) (announcement 7): Developers are responsible for generated output. Combine input/output filters or classifiers, abuse reporting/flagging, safety testing to industry standards, and red-team exercises. Include model upgrades and varied prompt scenarios in the safety process.
- [Tech Coalition: Developer good practices for combating online child sexual exploitation and abuse](https://technologycoalition.org/resources/developer-good-practices-combating-online-child-sexual-exploitation-and-abuse/) (announcements 9 and 14): Covers public prohibitions, internal moderation rules, in-app reports, risk-appropriate detection/human review, jurisdiction-specific reporting, lawful cooperation, safety by design, transparency reports, and user education. See the [bilingual child-safety topic](../age-restricted-child-safety/age-restricted-child-safety.en-US.md) for the guide's child links and access boundaries. This is industry guidance, not legal advice or a substitute for Play self-certification.
- [Play Academy: Build user trust with an accurate privacy policy](https://playacademy.exceedlms.com/student/activity/1961002-build-user-trust-with-an-accurate-privacy-policy) (announcement 1): The public course page says it was published June 18, 2026, lasts 2 minutes, and is Beginner level. Its description says the video helps users understand data handling and what to confirm before submitting a Data safety form. The logged-in video is unavailable publicly; binding privacy and disclosure duties come from User Data and Data safety.
- [Play Academy: SDK Best Practices](https://g.co/playacademy/sdkbestpractices) and [SDK Requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13323374) (announcement 22): Review SDK collection/sharing, permissions, and security behavior before integration. Continue monitoring SDK releases and vendor changes; the app developer remains responsible for violations caused by third-party code.
- [Designing your account deletion experience with users in mind](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2024/03/designing-your-account-deletion-experience-google-play.html) (announcement 17): Make deletion easy to find, automate it where possible, explain consequences in advance, be clear about cloud-data deletion timing, and let users submit a deletion request on the web after uninstalling the app. Handle payment cancellation, data export, lawful retention, and any reasonable recovery window clearly to prevent accidental deletion or the impression that data is gone when it is retained.
- [Boosting user privacy and business protection with updated Play policies](https://developer.android.com/blog/posts/boosting-user-privacy-and-business-protection-with-updated-play-policies) (announcement 2; current Android Developers page). On October 23, the announcement-linked original [Blogger URL](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/04/giving-users-clearer-choice-and-everyone-a-safer-more-trusted-app-ecosystem.html) was reopened directly and its 81-line body was accessible; the earlier HTTP 429 status is retained as historical audit evidence, not its current classification. The article recommends minimum contact/location scope and the official account-transfer workflow; credential sharing and third-party account sales are not permitted.
