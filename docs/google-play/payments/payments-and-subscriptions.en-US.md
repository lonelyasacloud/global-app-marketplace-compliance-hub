**Reviewed October 24, 2026.** This topic combines the Payments and Subscriptions requirements with their most relevant Help and implementation descendants. Google Play Billing is the default payment method for digital goods and services distributed through Play. Alternative billing and links to external payment are exceptions controlled by market, product, and program eligibility. Subscription disclosure, cancellation, and recurring-value rules apply independently, including to developers using alternative billing programs.

Apps Experience and revamped Level Up are voluntary programs and do not change baseline payment policy. See the bilingual program topic for reviewed program fees and market dates.

## 1. First classify the transaction

| Transaction/scenario | Summary |
| --- | --- |
| Paid app download on Play | Developers charging for a paid app downloaded through Google Play must use Google Play Billing for the download payment. |
| Digital in-app goods, features, content, and services | Google Play Billing is the default for digital goods, virtual currency/items, additional features, ad-free versions, cloud software, and subscriptions such as fitness, education, gaming, music, and video. Only listed policy exceptions or an enrolled program can change the payment method. |
| Physical goods/services | Google Play Billing must not be used for groceries, clothing, transport, flights, live events, and other physical goods/services. For mixed physical/digital SKUs, use Play Billing if the digital portion is greater or the SKU is marketed as a digital product. Physical businesses can use Google Pay or another compliant checkout where applicable. |
| P2P, some donations, bill payments | Policy exclusions include peer-to-peer payments, online auctions, tax-exempt donations, and credit-card/utility-bill remittances. A direct creator tip may count as P2P only if 100% goes to the creator and it grants no digital content or benefits, including badges or stickers. |
| Regulated clinical or live 1:1 services | Certain regulated clinical services and live one-to-one online services may qualify for exceptions when the complete Help-page conditions are met. A service must be between two individuals and not recorded, replayable, or reusable in any Play app. Do not assume all healthcare, consulting, or lessons are exempt. |
| Gift cards and earned rewards | In-app gift-card sales do not require Play Billing. Earned/rewarded points may be redeemed for digital goods, but sold points/virtual currency still require Play Billing. Virtual currency may only be used in the app/game title for which it was purchased. |
| Randomized paid virtual items | If a purchase can yield randomized virtual items, such as a loot box, disclose the odds before and close to the purchase flow. |

Google's Payments FAQ also says that a **consumption-only/reader** app can distribute content bought elsewhere, but cannot sell that content inside the app. It may give textual information about purchase options without a direct link. Marketing sent outside the app is treated differently from an in-app payment link. For bundles containing physical and digital benefits, assess the actual SKU composition and how it is marketed.

## 2. Limits on directing users to other payment methods

Except for the exceptions in Sections 3, 8, and 9 of the Payments policy, apps cannot direct users to non-Play payment for digital goods through the store listing, in-app promotions, WebViews, buttons, links, messages, ads, account-creation/sign-up flows, or other calls to action. Even text such as “the website is cheaper” can be a prohibited redirect when the app supports in-app purchases. Do not hide a payment route in a help page, pricing screen, or account-registration flow.

Alternative payment is not a blanket permission to add card checkout. In general, first verify the market, app, and user eligibility; enroll and receive approval in Play Console; implement the program's Billing/External Links APIs; show required information or choice screens; provide order/subscription management, refund, and support paths; meet PCI-DSS requirements when handling card data; and follow local law and child protections. Transaction reporting and service fees follow each program's terms.

### Chargeback cost sharing and Review Refund (orders dated after August 3, 2026)

Google's [refund-protection and chargeback-cost update](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17068375?hl=en) says that, for chargebacks on orders placed **after August 3, 2026**, developers bear the purchase price less the Play service fee and related financial-institution chargeback fees; Google continues to bear the Play service-fee cost. The date is already in effect and is based on the order date, not the date a dispute is processed.

Google offers an optional [Review Refund integration guide](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/provide-refund-and-chargeback-suggestions). After receiving a `PendingRefundReviewNotification` that requires developer review, the Android guide recommends calling [orders.reviewrefund](https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/api-ref/rest/v3/orders/reviewrefund) within 24 hours with a refund preference and purchase-usage evidence. Required fields include the pending refund token, whether a sample/trial/pre-purchase functionality information was provided, and the refund preference; consumption percentage and usage events are optional. This API submits a developer recommendation; it does not guarantee a dispute outcome, and its 24-hour response guidance should not be generalized to every refund request.

The workflow uses [real-time developer notifications](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/rtdn-reference): RTDN signals a state change, and the backend should call the Google Play Developer API for the complete state. The testing guide describes how license testers can simulate a purchase followed by a chargeback. API access also requires service-account/OAuth setup and Play Console permissions; Google's Getting Started guide lists viewing financial/order/cancellation-survey data and managing orders/subscriptions for Billing API access. See the operations register and policy link graph for the October 23, 2026 branch. Other RTDN event types and API descendants remain partly reviewed.

### Russia, Belarus, and sanctioned regions (dynamic snapshot)

Both Google's [international sanctions FAQ](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/11958934?hl=en) and its [Russia/Belarus billing article](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/11950272?hl=en) warn that conditions can change quickly. On **October 22, 2026**, the Help pages described the following effects; recheck the official pages and applicable local law before making publishing, payment, or subscription decisions:

- The sanctions FAQ says users in affected regions can still download free apps but cannot make purchases; previously downloaded/purchased content generally remains accessible, while subscriptions end after the current billing period. Developers whose payout bank is sanctioned or has suspended operations may need to update their payment profile with another bank or e-wallet that can receive payouts.
- The Russia/Belarus article records a Play Billing pause in Russia from March 10, 2022; free apps remain available. Russian users cannot purchase paid apps/games, subscriptions, or digital in-app purchases. The article also says Belarus users cannot purchase paid apps or subscriptions, while in-app purchases/subscriptions in free apps have a separately stated treatment. Paid app downloads and updates are blocked in Russia and Belarus; Russian users can keep using paid apps already downloaded to their devices.
- Russian subscriptions continue through the current billing period but cannot renew. Google lists a Play Console grace-period extension of up to 30 days or an API deferral of up to one year. The article's API link points to the deprecated legacy [`purchases.subscriptions.defer` method](https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/api-ref/rest/v3/purchases/subscriptions/defer); implementation should use [`purchases.subscriptionsv2.defer`](https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/api-ref/rest/v3/purchases.subscriptionsv2/defer) and verify current service availability and account access.
- The sanctions FAQ, Russia billing pause, and [suspension of seller services in Russia](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15685001?hl=en) describe distinct policy/service states. Do not collapse “users cannot purchase,” “developers cannot receive payouts,” and billing-policy exceptions into one rule. This section records Play Help snapshots and does not verify sanctions law, payment-profile, or account-specific status.

#### Suspension for Russia-based seller payout accounts (separate developer-side rule)

The [Suspension of seller services in Russia page](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15685001?hl=en) applies to Play developers whose **payout bank account is located in Russia**; it is not defined by developer nationality or user location. The page says Play monetization for this group was suspended indefinitely from December 26, 2024: December 25, 2024 (PST) was the last day to accrue global sales earnings, and the final balance was disbursed January 15, 2025 regardless of the configured payout threshold. Afterward, the affected account cannot earn from new paid-app, IAP, or subscription-renewal purchases through Play Billing. It may still publish or update free apps. Users can access only paid apps downloaded before December 26, 2024; an earlier purchase not downloaded by then is unavailable. Existing subscriptions continue through the current term, do not renew, and cancel on the next billing date; configured grace periods are honored, Console can extend access up to 30 days, and the API can defer renewal for up to one year. The Help page lists December 25, 2024 as the final refund date.

The linked [payout-threshold article](https://support.google.com/paymentscenter/answer/7163445) says ordinary monthly thresholds vary by country/currency and suggests considering at least US$100 for USD wire payouts to offset bank fees; that does not override the Russia page's final-payment date or no-threshold exception. The linked [Russia/Belarus billing-status article](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/11950272?hl=en) concerns user-market transaction restrictions and is distinct from the Russia payout-account suspension; the billing pause itself does not affect developer payouts. The seller page's [developer-support route](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/gethelp?utm_campaign=ru-by-seller-support-turndown-q4-2024&utm_medium=msa&utm_source=email) was blocked by Google's anti-bot screen, and its [general payments-policy link](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=ru) timed out. Check the developer payout bank, user-market purchase restrictions, sanctions FAQ, and current Console status separately.

For the merchant payout profile, see [Change or remove a merchant bank account](https://support.google.com/paymentscenter/answer/7162795?hl=en). It says a developer must add and verify another account and set it as primary before removing the only verified payout account. The Help page cannot confirm whether an account will pass sanctions screening or receive a payout.

The regional pages' Help sidebars also link to tax/region requirements and US/EEA payment programs. Those are category-navigation links; their program terms do not automatically apply to Russia or Belarus.

## 4. Subscription experience and cancellation requirements

- Describe the subscription benefits accurately in in-app promotions, splash screens, and plan-selection pages. Do not mislead users or use manipulative purchase flows.
- Before purchase, clearly disclose the subscription service/content, actual price, billing frequency, automatic renewal, whether the subscription is required to use the app, and other material terms. Users should not need another tap or expansion to discover the price or renewal details.
- Prominently show the actual amount charged and billing period. Official violation examples include presenting an annual/semiannual price mainly as a monthly breakdown, omitting the post-trial charge, hiding auto-renewal, incomplete localization, or obscuring the free/decline option.
- Free trials, promotional periods, introductory prices, and upgrade offers must explain the duration, the price and cycle afterward, and how to cancel. Discounts and one-time bonuses can supplement, but not replace, the recurring subscription value.
- Subscriptions must provide recurring value throughout the subscription term. A one-time currency bundle, gift pack, or single-use game booster belongs in a one-time product. Do not remove or materially weaken recurring value after purchase to turn a subscription into a one-time sale.
- Clearly explain management and cancellation in the app and provide an easy online cancellation method. Account settings can link to Google's Subscription Center for Play Billing subscriptions or provide direct access to the developer's cancellation flow.
- Cancellation usually takes effect after the current billing period, with access continuing until then; the current period is generally not refunded automatically. Some countries/laws allow immediate cancellation or prorated refunds. Developers must comply with applicable law and notify users of changes to subscription, cancellation, and refund terms.

### Entitlements and the subscription lifecycle: policy requirements vs. Play Billing behavior

This section separates **mandatory Play policy** from **Android Developers implementation guidance and Play Billing/API behavior**. The policy baseline is the disclosure, recurring-value, and cancellation requirements above. State names, notifications, configurable defaults, and API fields below come from implementation documentation; they do not create a universal requirement to enable any particular subscription type. The current English [Subscriptions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533?hl=en) opened directly in this review. Its `hl=zh-CN` URL was inaccessible. Android Developers uses `account hold` for the account state after a renewal payment failure; this section keeps the English term to avoid confusing it with data retention.

| Play Billing scenario/state | Behavior in the official implementation guide | Entitlement handling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New purchase and state sync | The lifecycle guide treats `purchases.subscriptionsv2.get` as the latest state source for subscription management; after a `SubscriptionNotification`, query this API. For a new purchase, the guide says to fetch and verify it on the backend and grant entitlement only when `subscriptionState` is `SUBSCRIPTION_STATE_ACTIVE`. | Do not use a client callback or notification type alone as the final entitlement decision; sync backend entitlements from the latest resource state. |
| Canceled but not yet expired | Play's Subscriptions policy says that, in general, a Play Billing cancellation takes effect after the current period and the user keeps content access for the remainder. The API resource can be `SUBSCRIPTION_STATE_CANCELED`; inspect each `lineItems[].expiryTime`: a future expiry means access continues, while a past expiry means entitlement should be removed immediately. The lifecycle guide warns against removing access while the user is still entitled. | “Canceled” does not mean “revoke access immediately.” If a developer shortens access for a refund, revocation, or other reason, follow the relevant process and applicable law/policy. |
| `SUBSCRIPTION_STATE_IN_GRACE_PERIOD` | Auto-renewing base plans have grace enabled by default; developers can change its length or disable it in Play Console. The guide says users **should retain** subscription entitlement during grace, and Play dynamically extends `expiryTime`. If the user fixes payment during grace, the renewal date stays the same. Even with a zero-day grace setting, Play has a 24-hour silent grace period; the state remains `ACTIVE` during that time, and Play sends an RTDN for the resulting state after it ends. | This is payment recovery behavior, separate from voluntary cancellation. The guide's “should” is implementation guidance, not another subscription-eligibility policy. |
| `SUBSCRIPTION_STATE_ON_HOLD` (`account hold`) | Auto-renewing base plans and installment plans have account hold enabled by default; its default duration is calculated as 60 days minus the grace-period duration, and it can be adjusted or disabled. The guide says to block entitlement in account hold. If the user fixes payment, the resource returns to `ACTIVE`, the same purchase token can be used, and the billing date resets to the recovery date. If the user does not recover before the hold ends, Play sends cancellation/expiration notifications. | Treat `SUBSCRIPTION_ON_HOLD` RTDN as a signal to re-fetch state from V2 `get`. The default duration is configurable, not an immutable guarantee for every account; shortening it may reduce the number of recovered subscriptions. |
| Pause: scheduled and effective | The guide says pause is enabled by default and can be disabled in Play Console. After a user schedules a pause, they retain access until the current paid period ends; once paused, the state is `SUBSCRIPTION_STATE_PAUSED`, access stops, and no renewal price is charged. The listed lengths are 1–4 weeks for weekly plans and 1–3 months for monthly, 3-month, 6-month, and annual plans; the page says options may change. | Pause is not an immediate cancellation. Play attempts to renew at the end of the pause; a payment failure moves directly to account hold. If the user resumes manually earlier, the billing date moves to that manual-resume date. |
| `SUBSCRIPTION_STATE_EXPIRED` | For an expired subscription, the guide says to remove entitlement and mark the purchase state invalid. | After RTDN, query the V2 resource and sync the backend rather than relying only on a local cache. |

#### Subscription types, the cancellation API, and plan/price changes

- **Prepaid plans:** They do not auto-renew. To continue entitlement without interruption, the user must top up the same subscription. Both the initial purchase and each top-up must be acknowledged: plans lasting at least one week must be acknowledged within three days; shorter plans within half of the plan duration. Missing the deadline can cause Play to revoke the top-up, revoke and cancel the remaining subscription, and issue a refund. These are Android Developers Billing integration requirements, not new disclosure-policy rules.
- **Installment cancellation:** If payments remain in the commitment period, a user-initiated cancellation takes effect at the end of that commitment period. The API first sends `SUBSCRIPTION_CANCELLATION_SCHEDULED`; the resource remains `ACTIVE` with `pendingCancellation`, and cancellation/expiration notifications follow only at the end of the commitment. Thus, the earlier statement about the current billing period does not cover the commitment period of an installment plan.
- **`purchases.subscriptionsv2.cancel` semantics:** The API requires `cancellationContext.cancellationType`. `USER_REQUESTED_STOP_RENEWALS` is user-requested, restorable, and stops only the next renewal; an installment user still finishes the commitment period. `DEVELOPER_REQUESTED_STOP_PAYMENTS` is developer-requested, cannot be restored, and stops the next payment; for installment plans, the user is not required to make the next payment or finish the commitment period. These API semantics do not replace the policy requirement for an easy online cancellation method, nor do they imply that every cancellation can immediately remove already-paid entitlement. See [`purchases.subscriptionsv2.cancel`](https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/api-ref/rest/v3/purchases.subscriptionsv2/cancel).
- **Upgrades, downgrades, and resubscribe:** Android Developers recommends clearly showing the user's current plan and the available change options. The Billing replacement mode determines when entitlement changes and how the paid value remaining in the current period is applied: `DEFERRED` switches at the old plan's expiry/renewal; other common modes can switch immediately and determine whether to prorate, charge immediately, or charge on renewal. Play blocks a plan change/resubscribe while the existing purchase is unacknowledged; a successful new purchase also needs acknowledgment. See the [subscriptions integration guide](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/subscriptions) for mode-specific behavior and constraints.
- **Price changes:** Changing a base-plan price does not affect existing subscribers by default; they remain in a legacy-price cohort unless the developer initiates migration. The default opt-in price increase requires the user to accept before the first renewal at the new price; otherwise Play automatically cancels the subscription. The current guide describes a 37-day advance period and says Play begins notifying users 30 days before their first renewal at the higher price. Opt-out increases are available only in eligible locations and subject to amount/frequency limits; notice periods vary by country between 30 and 60 days. For installment plans, price changes during a commitment take effect at the end of the active commitment period. Users in Korea must consent to a price step-up after a free-trial or introductory phase; lack of consent results in automatic cancellation. These are current Billing behaviors and a Korea-specific requirement, not a single global price-change flow. See the [subscription price changes guide](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/price-changes).

### Developer API subscription deferral: interface contract and policy boundary

This is a server-side Google Play Developer API method for moving a subscription's renewal date. The API reference defines the operation, not whether a developer is eligible to offer a particular extension or promotion. The current [Subscriptions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533?hl=en) still requires clear disclosure of subscription terms and sustained value throughout the subscription term; promotional bonuses may only supplement that recurring value. API success does not approve alternative billing, off-app payments, or another policy program; verify eligibility under the applicable program terms and account status separately.

- **Method and resource:** `POST https://androidpublisher.googleapis.com/androidpublisher/v3/applications/{packageName}/purchases/subscriptionsv2/tokens/{token}:defer`. Required path parameters are `packageName` (the package of the app where the subscription was purchased) and `token` (the token provided to the user's device at purchase). Unlike the legacy route, the V2 path does not require `subscriptionId`. It acts on the subscription purchase resource `SubscriptionPurchaseV2` in [`purchases.subscriptionsv2`](https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/api-ref/rest/v3/purchases.subscriptionsv2).
- **Request:** The JSON root contains required `deferralContext` of type `DeferralContext`. Its required `etag` must be the latest subscription etag, retrieved from the `SubscriptionPurchaseV2` returned by [`purchases.subscriptionsv2.get`](https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/api-ref/rest/v3/purchases.subscriptionsv2/get); the resource page says `etag` represents the current subscription state and is used for subscription actions. The API fails if the etag does not match the latest value. Required `deferDuration` is a Protocol Buffers [`Duration`](https://protobuf.dev/reference/protobuf/google.protobuf/) string: seconds, up to nine fractional digits, ending in `s`, for example `"3.5s"`. Optional `validateOnly: true` validates the effect without changing the subscription.
- **Response:** On success, `itemExpiryTimeDetails[]` returns objects of type `ItemExpiryTimeDetails`, each with `productId` and the new `expiryTime`. The time is an RFC 3339 Protocol Buffers [`Timestamp`](https://protobuf.dev/reference/protobuf/google.protobuf/) string; generated values are Z-normalized with 0, 3, 6, or 9 fractional digits, and the Timestamp format description also lists non-`Z` offsets as accepted forms.
- **Authorization:** OAuth scope `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/androidpublisher` is required.
- **Effect after success:** The [subscription lifecycle guide](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/lifecycle/subscriptions) says deferral sends a `SUBSCRIPTION_DEFERRED` RTDN and updates the renewal date; during the deferral the user retains full access to the content without being charged. For a subscription with add-ons, every item is deferred by the same duration. For prepaid plans, the API can defer the expiry time. The lifecycle example shows `SUBSCRIPTION_STATE_ACTIVE`; that is an outcome example, not a pre-call state restriction stated by the method reference.
- **State, retries, and limits:** The explicit pre-call state precondition in the method reference is a current `etag`. The V2 resource page defines a `subscriptionState` enum, but the defer method page does not specify which states are eligible for a call. The method page also does not list a maximum or cumulative duration, an idempotency key, or retry guarantees, so those constraints or guarantees cannot be asserted from it. The Russia/Belarus Help page's “up to one year” API guidance is a statement in that policy context; the new method reference itself does not state a one-year API limit.
- **Legacy replacement:** The old [`purchases.subscriptions.defer`](https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/api-ref/rest/v3/purchases.subscriptions/defer) method is explicitly marked deprecated and directs developers to V2. The old route includes `subscriptionId`; its `expectedExpiryTimeMillis` and `desiredExpiryTimeMillis` fields are Unix epoch milliseconds. Deferral fails if the current expiry differs from the expected value, and the desired expiry must be later than the current expiry. V2 instead uses a subscription token plus latest `etag` and relative duration, and returns expiry details for each subscription item rather than one `newExpiryTimeMillis` value.

## 5. Play Console product setup and technical implementation checks

In Play Console, subscriptions are configured as **subscription → base plan → offer**. Plan product IDs carefully because they cannot be changed or reused. A base plan sets billing period and regional availability/pricing; an offer configures acquisition/upgrade eligibility and phases such as trials or introductory pricing. The one-time product catalog is moving to a **one-time product → purchase option → offer** object model. Purchase options can define buy or rent terms and regional pricing/availability; offers can define pre-orders, discounts, or Play Games rewards. Some features are in an Early Access Program (EAP), so check eligibility for the specific feature.

Google's typical Billing flow is: show the product → launch the Play purchase flow → verify the purchase on a secure backend → grant entitlement only after the purchase is complete → notify the user → acknowledge processing to Google; consume consumables so they can be purchased again. Do not grant entitlement while a transaction is pending or before verification, and do not grant it twice for one purchase token. Google requires acknowledgment within **three days**; otherwise the purchase may be automatically refunded and entitlement revoked. Google recommends secure-backend verification of purchase tokens and Real-time Developer Notifications to keep subscription state synchronized.

For alternative-payment programs, also verify Play APIs for information screens/parental controls, UX requirements for developer-rendered choice screens, 24-hour transaction reports, external-destination/privacy rules, subscription/order management URLs, and pre-release Console declarations. ## 6. Pre-release checklist

1. Classify each SKU as digital/physical, note where it is consumed, payment route, and user market; review mixed SKUs against both composition and marketing.
2. Find every external-payment phrase, link, WebView, button, landing page, account-creation path, and ad CTA. Keep an external route only when the app is enrolled in the matching approved market program.
3. For any out-of-Play payment, record program, package, market, form factor, choice screen, enrollment, API/reporting status, and applicable fees. Track the December 1, 2026 US ECL reporting/fee deadline and March 31, 2027 Japan program transition.
4. Review every subscription plan for actual charge, cycle, auto-renewal, trial conversion, eligibility, localization, recurring value, cancellation, and refund information.
5. Test completed/pending purchases, refunds/chargebacks, restore purchases, cross-device use, subscription pause/grace/hold/expiry, and API notifications. Confirm unverified or PENDING purchases do not grant entitlement.
6. Align Data safety, privacy disclosures, financial-feature declarations, child protections, store listing, and checkout with actual payment/data processing. See the separate Financial Services topic for licensing and financial-product declarations.

### Billing backend, choice UX, and testing descendants

The Android Developers pages below are implementation and testing guidance. They do not grant eligibility for any alternative-billing or off-app program. Program terms still control eligible markets, fees, enrollment, and account status.

- **Backend verification and entitlement sync:** Build a server-side purchase-status system and use RTDN to react to purchase/subscription state changes; deduplicate by `messageId`. Verify purchase tokens through the Developer API before granting entitlements; never grant while a purchase is `PENDING`. Acknowledge/consume after granting valid purchases, and monitor voided/refunded/charged-back purchases for entitlement revocation. Server acknowledgement protects against client connectivity failures or tampering; purchases not acknowledged within three days may be automatically refunded.
- **Subscription/API lifecycle:** The [Google Play Developer API](https://developers.google.com/android-publisher) covers subscription and one-time purchase resources; see the “Developer API subscription deferral” note above for the method path, request/response types, OAuth scope, etag precondition, and post-success effect. The old `purchases.subscriptions.defer` is explicitly marked deprecated on its API reference page and points to V2.
- **RTDN lifecycle:** [Purchase lifecycle and RTDNs](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/lifecycle) describes receiving events through Pub/Sub, decoding message data, deduplicating by unique `messageId`, then updating entitlements based on one-time purchase/subscription events. It advises against relying only on polling. The page links setup, push/pull, RTDN message schema, one-time purchase, and subscription lifecycle guides; deeper API descendants were not individually traversed.
- **Fraud controls and content delivery:** [Fight fraud and abuse](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/security) recommends keeping sensitive validation on the server, treating purchase tokens as unique identifiers, and validating through the Developer API. Grant only when state is `PURCHASED`; do not acknowledge/consume rejected transactions; explicitly refund/revoke when appropriate; reconcile linked tokens and voided purchases. Do not use order ID as the unique key. Digital content can be delivered from a server/realtime service rather than bundling extractable full content in the APK.
- **Testing matrix:** The [Billing test guide](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/test) and [application licensing guide](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6062777) recommend license testers, test tracks, and Play Billing Lab for approved/declined, repeat purchases, pending/cancelled purchases, chargebacks, subscription renewal/grace-period/pause scenarios. Non-license testers on test tracks may be charged real money. License-tester accounts may expose pause/resubscribe features even when disabled in production Console, so verify disabled behavior with a non-license-tester account. Test purchases that are not acknowledged may be refunded after three minutes; some Billing Lab configurations expire after about two hours.
- **Fair billing-choice presentation:** The [developer-rendered choice-screen guidelines](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/billingchoice/ux-guidelines) require equal, nearby presentation of Play Billing and alternative options (button size, text, contrast, branding); users must be able to identify the alternative provider. Retrieve Google Play payment methods from the API each time and do not cache them. Clearly disclose when a link takes users out of the app. The UI guide applies only within an eligible enrolled program and is not a standalone permission to link users off-app.
- **User Choice Billing:** Use only after the relevant program eligibility, enrollment, and Console settings are satisfied. The client enables `enableUserChoiceBilling` and registers a `UserChoiceBillingListener`. When the user’s Play country and program configuration qualify, Play shows the choice screen. A Play selection continues through standard Play Billing; an alternative selection returns an `externalTransactionToken`, which the app should pass securely to its backend before starting the developer’s checkout. The backend must report a completed transaction through the Developer API within 24 hours. Upgrades/downgrades of an alternative-billed subscription stay on the original billing system and reference its original `externalTransactionId`; check current program notices for manual-reporting migration deadlines.
- **Alternative Billing only:** This flow does not offer a choice between Play Billing and the alternative system. Enable `enableAlternativeBillingOnly`, call `isAlternativeBillingOnlyAvailableAsync`, and proceed only when it returns `OK`. Call Play’s information dialog before starting the purchase; proceed only when the dialog call succeeds. Generate a new token for each one-time purchase, new subscription, and subscription upgrade/downgrade, then report through the backend within 24 hours. Do not continue an off-app transaction when the feature is unavailable, unsupported on the device, or returns an error.
- **EEA External Offers:** This is an external link purchase/app-download flow, not in-app alternative billing. After meeting current terms and receiving approval, enable `BillingProgram.EXTERNAL_OFFER` and check availability for the user with `isBillingProgramAvailableAsync`. Generate a fresh token immediately before each exit; do not cache tokens across transactions. Call `launchExternalLink` on the main thread and handle any Play information screen. An app-download destination must be registered and approved in Play Console and use `LAUNCH_IN_EXTERNAL_BROWSER_OR_APP`. If the app opens the URI itself, launch it only after an `OK` callback; the URI must not contain personally identifiable information. Report applicable transactions through the backend Developer API. For multiple purchases within one external-offer flow, the official integration guide allows the same token; each transaction still needs its own unique transaction ID.
- **Version and UX scope:** The Alternative Billing only overview says Billing Library 6.2.1+, while its detailed integration guide says 6.1+, so the official pages conflict. The External Offers overview and integration page both say 8.2.1+, and the UCB guide says 5.2+. Versions can change; check the current Play Billing release notes before implementation. When the corresponding client APIs are used, they provide the information/choice screens required by the guide, so separate legacy interim-UX guidance does not apply. For manually rendered flows, check the interim UX page for the relevant market. API implementation does not replace program eligibility or enrollment approval.
- **External transaction reporting constraints:** Start and report transactions only for eligible users, apps, and programs; unsupported country/program requests are rejected. Events include initial purchases, subscription renewals, prepaid top-ups, upgrades/downgrades, refunds, and app downloads; free trials and app installs are reported at zero value. Initial purchases, first subscription events, and downloads require the token supplied through Play Billing Library plus a new unique `externalTransactionId`. Each later renewal/top-up gets a new unique ID and references the initial transaction ID; no token is needed for subsequent events. Product changes, or repurchases after cancellation/expiry, need a new ID. IDs must contain no personal, proprietary, or confidential information. Refunds reference the exact transaction/recurrence being refunded; partial refunds use the pre-tax amount.
- **External app downloads and tax fields:** The first External Offers transaction must include `ExternalOfferDetails`. Report an app download as a zero-cost one-time transaction with `LINK_TO_APP_DOWNLOAD` and the installed package/category. Transactions inside a downloaded external app reuse the download-event token and set `appDownloadEventExternalTransactionId` to link to the original download. For India, include `userTaxAddress.administrativeArea` when tax depends on state/province. Migration from legacy manual reporting is limited to active subscriptions from developers who used that legacy path: create a zero-cost record with the original signup time and `migratedTransactionProgram`; the field will be deprecated when manual reporting ends and is not for ordinary new transactions.
- **Quotas and partner fields:** The ExternalTransactions API sets a 1,200 QPM limit on calls to `createexternaltransaction` and `refundexternaltransaction`; `getexternaltransaction` is excluded, and the page does not say whether the two write methods use separate buckets. General Developer API quotas use separate buckets; the public quota page lists a default of 3,000 QPM per bucket. Only eligible apps in partner programs such as Media Experience need `transactionProgramCode`; do not set it for External Offers transactions. The quota-increase form target could not be resolved in this pass, so no increase or approval is assumed.

### Recent subscription changes: backward compatibility and one-way migration

[Recent changes to subscriptions in Play Console](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12124625?hl=en) describes the subscription-model transition introduced in 2022: separating Subscription from Base plan/Offer, with support for prepaid plans, upgrade offers, and custom eligibility. The article says existing subscriptions are converted into a base plan and compatible offer; older app/API clients can continue to receive a compatible view but cannot use every new capability. If a developer clicks “Make subscription editable” in Play Console to migrate a legacy product, the migration is one-way; the old `inappproducts` API can no longer read, configure, or update that subscription. Before migrating, review legacy clients, promo codes, featured subscriptions, and Subscribe with Google compatibility, and preserve the compatible offer used by older clients until their share is acceptable.

The article also says country availability settings affect future supported markets, so check whether new countries should be enabled for each product. The legacy `inappproducts` API remains available for products not migrated but lacks new subscription capabilities. The page links to subscription-object overviews, Play Console subscription management, legacy APIs, migration/integration guides, and the Monetization Subscriptions API. This summary does not cover every Console/API change made after the article; check current API documentation before implementation.

### EEA distribution “flexibility”: a waiver for specified contract terms only

[Flexibility for EEA app distribution](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16232690?hl=en) and its [previous contract changes article](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/16125693?hl=en) describe a waiver in the context of the Digital Markets Act (DMA). Google sent notices to developers whose agreements might contain most-favoured-nation/price-parity (MFN) terms. The waiver took effect on **January 1, 2024** for developers offering products/services to EEA end users through Play, regardless of the developer's business location. It removes only the relevant MFN clauses; the rest of the agreement remains in force. Notice details are generally available only to businesses that received the notice; the Help article provides play-bd-ops@google.com as a contact.

This branch links to the European Commission's [official Digital Markets Act page](https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/index_en). The DMA explanation and contract waiver do not enroll a developer in alternative billing, External Offers, external distribution, or another Play program. Each program retains its own market, eligibility, API, reporting, and fee terms.
