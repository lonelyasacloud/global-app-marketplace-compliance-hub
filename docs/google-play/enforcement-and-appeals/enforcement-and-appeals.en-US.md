**Reviewed October 22, 2026.** This topic explains the main app/account enforcement actions, appeals, and adjacent routes. The developer's case notice, current Play Console status, and applicable contract/policy control an individual case.

## Review scope and developer responsibility

Google may consider store metadata, the in-app experience, account history, integrated third-party code/SDKs, user reports, and proactive review. Developers remain responsible for third-party code and its practices in their apps. An enforcement notice may not list every issue in an app catalog; review the affected app and the rest of the account catalog rather than fixing only the most visible symptom.

Policies apply beyond APK/AAB files: they cover content apps display or link to, ads, hosted or linked UGC, and public developer information such as the developer name and website landing page. See [Policy Coverage](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10146128?hl=en), the [Developer Program Policy](../developer-program-policy/developer-program-policy.en-US.md), and the [Developer Distribution Agreement](../developer-distribution-agreement/developer-distribution-agreement.en-US.md).

## Enforcement actions

| Action | Main impact | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rejection | A new app or update is not published. If an update is rejected, the previously published version generally stays available; a rejection alone does not affect account standing. | Fix every cited issue before submitting again. |
| Removal | The app and earlier versions are removed; the listing can return after a compliant update is approved. A removal does not immediately affect account standing, but multiple removals may lead to suspension. In-app purchase/billing functionality may be unavailable while removed. | Do not republish until all violations are fixed. |
| Suspension | The app and versions are removed, and the suspended app's APK/AAB cannot be used. A suspension counts as a strike; multiple or egregious violations can terminate the account and related accounts. | Review account-wide status and related apps, not only the suspended package. |
| Limited visibility | The app remains accessible by direct store link but has reduced Play discoverability; this alone does not affect account standing. | Review the notice and listing; a working direct link does not mean visibility limits are lifted. |
| Limited regions | Users can download only in specified regions. Existing users may keep using the app but no longer receive updates; account standing is not affected by the region restriction itself. | Check country targeting and the exact regions in the notice. |
| Restricted developer account | Apps in the catalog are removed, and the developer cannot publish new apps or republish existing ones; Play Console access remains for providing information and fixing issues. | Resolve account-level and catalog-wide issues in the Console. |
| Account termination | Catalog apps are removed and the developer cannot publish; related developer accounts may be permanently suspended. Do not try to evade termination by opening another account; new accounts can also be terminated without a registration-fee refund. | See [account termination](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/2491922) and the applicable appeal deadline. |

The [Enforcement Process](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899234?hl=en) says actions generally apply in all regions and remain in effect unless a notice says otherwise, until the action is appealed successfully or the issue is fixed.

## Check Policy status in Play Console

[Check your app’s policy status](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9842754?hl=en) says this page shows whether a review found a violation of the Developer Program Policies or DDA and any active enforcement, such as rejection, removal, or suspension. The path is Play Console → select an app → **Policy status** in the left menu. This is an account-specific app view, not a public lookup page.

- “No issues found” means no current enforcement action is shown for that app; it does not certify every future version or unreviewed use case.
- If an update is rejected, the last successfully published version generally stays available. A removed app returns only after a compliant update is submitted. A suspended app is unavailable and may be appealed through the route in the notice.
- Do not republish a rejected or removed version before fixing the cited violation.
- A resubmission after removal requires a release-track sweep: deactivate noncompliant app bundles, update or discard drafts across production/open/closed/internal tracks as applicable, and ensure the violating version is no longer included in each release. Otherwise resubmission can fail and live bundles may be removed.
- Future subscriptions are canceled after removal, with no refund of past subscription charges solely because of the removal. Users, statistics, and ratings are retained when a compliant update for a removed app is approved. A suspension forfeits the removed app’s users, statistics, and ratings.

App-level status, account restrictions, and termination notices may use different routes. Use the [official enforcement and appeals guide](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899142?hl=en), [app removal/resubmission article](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/2477981?hl=en), and the case-specific notice; do not infer case eligibility from public Help pages alone.

## Appeals, remediation, and deadlines

1. **Read the full notice and check the status.** Rejection, removal, suspension, and warning can have different remediation and appeal paths. Google does not promise a warning before suspension or termination. A warning means the app is close to suspension; ignoring it or launching a second app with the same issue increases risk. See [Fair warnings](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/2985876).
2. **Use the route in the notice.** [Managing Policy Violations and Appeals](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899142?hl=en&ref_topic=9877468) says to appeal through the notice email link; Google says it will reinstate an app when it finds an error. Another route may be to fix the issue and submit a compliant version. The interactive [contact/appeal troubleshooter](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/troubleshooter/2993242) asks whether the case concerns an account termination or an app, the cited reason, and whether the action is believed to be in error. It is a routing form, not a public statement of substantive review criteria.
3. **Account terminations have a defined deadline.** [Account termination appeal time limit](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16659089?hl=en&ref_topic=3453554) says appeals must be submitted within **180 days** of termination starting **January 28, 2026**. After that the appeal option closes. A one-time grace period for earlier terminations ended **July 27, 2026**; that date has passed as of this review. An appeal filed near the deadline receives an additional 30 days after the decision to submit new information for reconsideration. The termination article also says one appeal may be filed per account termination. Do not automatically apply this account-termination deadline to every app-level appeal.
4. **Distinguish account termination from app removal.** [My app has been removed](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/2477981) summarizes rejection, removal, suspension, warnings, and subscription effects. [Account termination](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/2491922) explains that repeated suspensions or serious violations may terminate an account and warns against opening a new account to evade action.

## Adjacent routes that are not policy appeals

- **Play Protect classification or sideloading warning:** [App verification](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/2992033) and its [Play Protect appeal form](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/contact/protectappeals) address warnings or blocks for potentially harmful apps installed from the internet or third-party markets. This is separate from a Play Store rejection, removal, or account termination appeal. The form requests the package name, the uploaded file's VirusTotal SHA-256, and supporting information; it says decisions are final, does not provide future-compliance advice, and states that it will not send case-by-case responses.
- **Reporting another app or review:** [Report a policy violation](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/1085703) is for reporting another app or content that violates the DDA/policies; copyright, trademark, and review reports have separate routes. [Report inappropriate reviews](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/7318385) requires a review-policy violation; if a review is found compliant, it cannot be reported again, and daily account limits may apply. These are not appeals of your own app's enforcement.
- **Developer identity is a different issue.** Account/package registration requirements are in [Play Console Requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10788890?hl=en&ref_topic=9877468) and the Developer Verification topic (snapshot: `../android-developer-verification-en.md`, not imported in this repository). App verification appeal deals with Play Protect's harmful-app classification.

## Additional mediation route for eligible EEA developers

The [General Conditions of Access for Google Play in the EEA §G](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14659200) say that if a dispute about Google's application of Play policies to an app offered through Play to EEA users remains unresolved after Google's internal appeal, a developer meeting the EEA conditions may apply to the [Google Play Mediation Scheme (GPMS)](https://www.cedr.com/mediation-services/schemes/platform-to-business-services/google-play-mediation-scheme). CEDR describes GPMS as Google's mechanism under DMA Article 6(12). That article requires covered gatekeepers to apply fair, reasonable, and non-discriminatory general access conditions for business users and to publish conditions that include an alternative dispute settlement mechanism. The [official EUR-Lex DMA text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX%3A32022R1925) is linked for direct checking. Do not conflate GPMS with the P2B Help Center regional notice linked from the Policy Center: that notice only says P2B Help content is limited to EU-established business users and unavailable in some regions; it does not give GPMS procedures or decide an individual case. GPMS is also not for consumer purchase complaints.

### GPMS application and preparation

The opened [CEDR application form](https://forms.cedr.com/240441968516966) is a public page; no form was submitted. It asks for contact details, a facts/issues summary of no more than 500 words, and availability within 15–45 business days after submission. CEDR contacts Google to check the internal-appeal status; the current scheme page gives Google 10 business days to agree or report the appeal status. The process proceeds only if both parties agree. Within 5 business days after mediator appointment, the mediator contacts both sides to set the document and procedure timetable. CEDR says mediation is usually online and usually scheduled 30–45 business days after the initial application. Up to 7 hours are allocated per case (typically about 3 hours of preparation and a 4-hour session), flexibly divided by the mediator. These are current process descriptions, not a guarantee that an individual case will finish on a fixed date.

The [Case Summary Guidance and Template](https://www.cedr.com/hubfs/Commercial%20Documents/Scheme%20Documents/Google/Google%20Play/Google%20Play%20Mediation%20Case%20Summary%20Preparation%20Guidance%20and%20Template.pdf) recommends a summary of no more than 10 pages including appendices, exchanged about 10 business days before the joint session. It should state the facts, issues, and desired outcome; useful supporting material can include a chronology, key correspondence, relevant policies/terms, Google's internal appeal decision, attendee roles, and a glossary. The scheme page says to include all facts and issues the party wants considered and warns that the summary is shared with both the mediator and the other party. Attendees should know the issues and have authority to resolve them. The agreement and supporting documents are normally in English; CEDR says foreign-language support can be arranged with both parties' agreement.

The [Mediation Agreement](https://www.cedr.com/hubfs/Commercial%20Documents/Scheme%20Documents/Google/Google%20Play/Google-Play-Mediation-Scheme-Mediation-Agreement-March.pdf) is signed before mediation and sets the mediator's role, confidentiality, and no-recording process. The linked 2023 [Model Mediation Procedure](https://www.cedr.com/hubfs/Commercial%20Documents/Scheme%20Documents/Google/Google%20Search/CEDR-Model-Mediation-Procedure-2023.pdf) is CEDR's generic procedure, covering referral, mediator selection, preparation, document exchange, agreement, online/in-person private and joint meetings, confidentiality, conclusion, and complaints; it is not a Google Play product policy. It cautions that a document otherwise disclosable in proceedings does not automatically gain privilege merely because it is first produced in mediation; assess sensitive disclosures and seek legal advice. The 2023 [Code of Conduct for Third-Party Neutrals](https://www.cedr.com/hubfs/Commercial%20Documents/Scheme%20Documents/Google/Google%20Search/CEDR-Code-of-Conduct-for-Third-Party-Neutrals-2023.pdf) focuses on independence, impartiality, competence, and conflict disclosure.

The mediator facilitates discussion and does not issue an adjudication or formal recommendation; without agreement the process ends without a resolution. CEDR's current GPMS page says an agreed outcome is written into a final, binding settlement agreement; Google's [EEA Conditions §G](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14659200) say the mediation outcome is not binding. Read these as distinguishing the mediator's lack of power to decide from a separate settlement document signed by both parties; check the signed terms and applicable law in an individual case. Google pays scheme/mediator fees, while the developer bears its own legal, preparation, and attendance costs. Other rights under applicable law remain. See recursive link graph §4.95 (snapshot: `../google-play-policy-link-graph-en.md`, not imported in this repository) for sources and descendant-link status.

## Long-inactive accounts

[Closure of inactive developer accounts](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/11605267) describes two criteria. An account with no apps may be considered inactive when it is over one year old and has never submitted an app for review. For an account with apps, all of these must apply: the account is over one year old; all published apps (including removed/suspended apps) have fewer than 1,000 combined lifetime installs; the contact email and phone are unverified; and the account has not been used in Play Console for 180 days. Google sends email and Console notices with 60/30/7-day reminders. Verify contact details and complete the app-upload/publishing step by the notice deadline. If an app is not ready for public release, the page also lists operational routes such as [Create and set up your app](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859152?hl=en), [Internal App Sharing](https://google.play/business/internalappsharing/), [Internal testing](https://google.play/business/internal-testing/), and [Closed testing](https://google.play/business/closed-testing/). These are release/testing tools, not exemptions from a separate closed-testing gate that may apply to eligible new Personal accounts; see the [release workflow topic](../publishing/play-release-workflow.en-US.md). The registration fee is not refunded.
