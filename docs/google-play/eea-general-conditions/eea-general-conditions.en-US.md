## Scope and contractual relationship

- The developer relationship remains grounded in the globally applicable [Developer Distribution Agreement (DDA)](../developer-distribution-agreement/developer-distribution-agreement.en-US.md) and Developer Program Policies. This page is not a replacement for the DDA; it collects account onboarding, service-fee information, EEA programs, and appeal/mediation routes.
- Developers participating in EEA-specific payment or External Offers programs must follow those program terms and ensure enrolled apps are not offered to users outside the EEA.
- Section A lists Play Console registration, acceptance of the [DDA](../developer-distribution-agreement/developer-distribution-agreement.en-US.md) and Play Console Terms of Service (snapshot: `play-console-terms-en.md`, not imported in this repository), the one-time $25 registration fee, account type, identity verification, and applicable new-Personal-account testing requirements. These are account/launch prerequisites, not new DMA age or content duties for every EEA app.
- Section B is product information about safety review, developer tools, distribution infrastructure, acquisition, and engagement. These claims and feature links do not create additional policy obligations; check each product guide and account state separately.

## Fees, alternative billing, and External Offers

### Service-fee basis

EEA fees depend on transaction type, whether Play Billing is used, program participation, and whether a user is a new or existing install. This page uses **June 30, 2026** as the cutover: a user is a new install if their first Play install or first Play update occurred on or after that date; all others are existing installs. Its fee table varies by subscription versus other digital transactions, install status, and program tier. Rates change, so check the [Payments and subscriptions topic](../payments/payments-and-subscriptions.en-US.md), current Play Console invoices, and the linked Help pages before pricing or release. Do not infer account eligibility for a fee tier from the public page alone.

### The alternative-billing routes are different

- **User Choice Billing (UCB):** In eligible regions, users choose between Google Play Billing and alternative billing. Program requirements, UX, API integration, reporting, and fees are summarized in the [Payments and subscriptions topic](../payments/payments-and-subscriptions.en-US.md).
- **EEA alternative billing:** This opt-in program allows enrolled businesses to offer alternative billing only inside their EEA app, without also offering Google Play Billing. Payment security, user support, dispute handling, disclosures, and transaction reporting requirements apply.
- **Parent/child fee-text mismatch:** Section E of this general-conditions page still says the EEA alternative-only program pays the standard service fee less three percentage points. The linked current [EEA alternative billing Help article](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12348241?hl=en) says the June 30, 2026 fee schedule instead distinguishes new/existing installs and transaction types; the three-point adjustment is the earlier rule. Follow the current program Help and Console invoices. The [Payments topic](../payments/payments-and-subscriptions.en-US.md) records this discrepancy.

### External Offers

External Offers lets enrolled developers show EEA users in-app links to their own external offers. Enrollment and approval are required before promotion. The program covers destinations, app downloads, the notice that a user is leaving Play, malware/seriously harmful content, customer support, refunds/disputes, and reporting authorized transactions within 24 hours. The linked current page states that, from **June 4, 2026**, the initial acquisition fee is 0%; standard ongoing fees include 10% for auto-renewing subscriptions, 20% for other digital items/services, and €1.20 per app download (subject to adjustment). The newer Apps & Games tiers and the first-$1-million earnings tier differ. Verify current eligibility and rates in [External Offers terms](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14372887?hl=en) and the Payments topic.

## Appeals, EEA mediation, and leaving Google Play

- A developer may first appeal a Google policy-enforcement action internally. If a dispute about applying Play policies to an app offered to EEA users remains unresolved, a developer who meets the EEA General Conditions may apply to the CEDR-administered [Google Play Mediation Scheme (GPMS)](https://www.cedr.com/mediation-services/schemes/platform-to-business-services/google-play-mediation-scheme). This mechanism implements the alternative dispute settlement requirement in DMA Article 6(12); it is distinct from the P2B Help Center regional notice and does not handle consumer purchase complaints. Both sides must agree to mediate; the mediator does not issue a binding adjudication. CEDR says an agreed outcome is documented in a final, signed settlement agreement, while Google's EEA conditions say the mediation outcome is not binding. The library therefore distinguishes the mediator's lack of decision-making power from a settlement the parties sign; check the signed text and applicable law in an individual case. Google pays the scheme/mediator fee; the developer pays its own legal, preparation, and attendance costs. Participation does not remove other remedies under applicable law. The [Enforcement and appeals topic](../enforcement-and-appeals/enforcement-and-appeals.en-US.md) summarizes the route, CEDR form, and process documents.
- Section H says developers should unpublish software, content, and other items/services made available through Play Console, then stop using Play Console and related developer credentials. DDA Section 10 still applies. Leaving Console does not automatically erase prior transaction, refund, support, or legal duties.

## Before using an EEA program

1. Confirm the target user territory and business model, then determine whether UCB, EEA alternative billing, or External Offers applies. Verify the program selection, approval, and transaction reporting in Play Console.
2. Track transaction territory, new/existing install status, transaction type, Play Billing use, and program eligibility separately. External Offers, alternative billing, and the 2024 EEA MFN contract waiver are different mechanisms.
3. If this general page differs from current program Help on rates or dates, check the current program terms, Console invoices, and developer notices; preserve the discrepancy and review date in the knowledge base.

This page is a bilingual paraphrase, not the complete contract or legal advice. Google says the mediation outcome itself is not binding; CEDR says the mediator does not adjudicate and a mutually agreed settlement is written and signed. The library preserves this wording difference and does not infer the effect of a signed settlement in an individual case from the phrase “mediation outcome”; check the signed terms and applicable law.
