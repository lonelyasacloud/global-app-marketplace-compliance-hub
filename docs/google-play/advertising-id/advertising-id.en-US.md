## Choose the right identifier

| Use case | Preferred option / limit | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ad delivery, attribution, and ad-fraud checks | Use AAID when the device provides it; do not substitute IMEI, SSAID, MAC, or another device identifier for advertising | Respect ad-personalization choices, reset/deletion behavior, and disclosure requirements |
| Analytics or fraud prevention unrelated to ads | Android guidance suggests considering App Set ID, Firebase Installation ID, or an app-private GUID | App Set ID cannot be used for ads personalization or measurement; FID/GUID have narrower scope |
| Device authenticity / abuse prevention | Consider Play Integrity API | It is not a cross-app advertising identifier and does not remove disclosure duties |

## Current Play requirements for AAID

- **Purpose limitation:** AAID is only for advertising and user analytics. Check the user's interest-based-advertising or ads-personalization opt-out setting every time the ID is accessed.
- **Analytics association limits:** Do not connect AAID to personally identifiable information or persistent device identifiers for analytics purposes.
- **Advertising association limits:** Do not connect AAID to persistent device identifiers for advertising. Connecting it to personally identifiable information for advertising requires the user's explicit consent.
- **Respect resets:** After a reset, do not connect a new AAID to an earlier AAID or data derived from it without explicit user consent.
- **Respect opt-out and deletion:** After opt-out, do not use AAID to build advertising profiles or target personalized ads. Contextual ads, frequency capping, conversion tracking, reporting, and security/fraud detection remain listed as allowed activities. On newer devices, deletion returns zeros; do not reconnect a device without an AAID to data linked to or derived from the previous AAID.
- **Disclosure and SDKs:** Disclose collection and use in a legally adequate privacy notification. The developer remains responsible for SDKs and business partners that receive AAID. Keep Data safety, the privacy policy, and actual SDK data flows consistent.
- **Use the advertising identifier for ads:** When AAID is available, apps on Google Play must use it instead of other device identifiers for advertising purposes.

## Children and mixed audiences

Check Families restrictions separately: child-only apps must not transmit children's identifiers; child-only apps targeting API 33 or higher must not request AD_ID. Mixed-audience apps must not transmit identifiers from children or users of unknown age. Review audience routing, third-party SDKs, ad mediation, and actual data flows. A “standard advertising ID” does not override Families or User Data requirements. See [Age restrictions and child safety](../age-restricted-child-safety/age-restricted-child-safety.en-US.md) and the [Families policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en).

## Android implementation checks

- Apps targeting Android 13/API 33 or higher and using AAID through Google Play services need the normal permission **com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID** in the final merged manifest. An ads SDK may contribute it through its library manifest; inspect Android Studio's Merged Manifest.
- Without AD_ID, the relevant API returns zeros. It is a normal install-time permission, not a runtime dangerous permission.
- **AdvertisingIdClient.getAdvertisingIdInfo(Context)** must not be called on the main thread. Handle missing Play services, connection failures, and recoverable errors. When reading **Info.getId()**, also check **Info.isLimitAdTrackingEnabled()** and honor the user's setting.
- Retrieve the identifier when needed instead of caching it. Define behavior for no Play services, no ID, opt-out, reset, and deletion.
- Use App Set ID only for essential non-ad analytics/fraud prevention. Play-installed apps may share a developer-scoped ID; non-Play installs, an unknown developer account, or no Play services result in app scope. The ID may reset after more than 13 months without access, uninstalling the last associated app, or a factory reset. Retrieve it through the SDK when needed; do not rely on a cached value.

### Android unique-identifier guide: use-case matrix and direct descendants

On October 22, 2026, I reopened the full current [Android Developers best-practices guide for unique identifiers](https://developer.android.com/identity/user-data-ids). The page was last updated **October 16, 2026**. I attempted all **31 body-link anchors** (IDs 15–45); repeated policy/API destinations were grouped by their actual targets. One Universal Analytics `Tracker.html` method link returned a cache miss. The footer's [Android Developers Content License](https://developer.android.com/license) was opened separately. This is Android implementation guidance; it does not expand Play policy or establish review approval or a policy exemption.

The core advice is to choose the **narrowest-scope, most resettable** identifier that meets the product need and avoid non-resettable hardware IDs. Use AAID for advertising, honor user opt-outs, and never bridge a reset. For most non-ad uses, use an app-instance FID or a private GUID. Evaluate scope, persistence/reset behavior, uniqueness, and spoofing/non-repudiation together rather than optimizing only for uniqueness. Since Android 10, access to IMEI/serial is restricted to device/profile owners, carrier-privileged apps, or privileged permission holders. Third-party apps cannot read the device MAC on Android 6+; Android 11 Passpoint MACs are randomized per configuration, and network-information needs should prefer higher-level APIs such as `ConnectivityManager`.

| Use case | Guide recommendation / platform reference | Relationship to Play policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carrier telephony or SMS features | IMEI/IMSI/Line1 may be relevant when genuinely required for carrier functionality; ordinary apps should prefer a user sign-in and retrieving carrier details server-side, and gracefully handle permission denial. | Android implementation advice. Play User Data and Sensitive Permissions apply separately; carrier exceptions must still satisfy platform access conditions. |
| SIM/subscription status | Use `SubscriptionInfo` / Subscription ID; `READ_PHONE_STATE` is one access requirement. | The guide recommends migrating from ICCID, a globally unique, non-resettable identifier whose access is restricted on newer Android versions. Check the API reference for device/API compatibility. |
| Single sign-on | Use an account-manager-compatible account link and request only the OAuth scopes needed. | Account linking is an authentication identity, not an advertising ID, and does not grant Play permission to access sensitive data. The linked desktop OAuth guide treats local clients as public clients that cannot keep secrets and recommends PKCE. |
| Ad targeting, ad measurement, conversion, remarketing, and ad-fraud checks | Use resettable AAID; for measurement/conversion, Play Install Referrer APIs may also be relevant. | Play's Advertising ID policy says not to substitute a non-resettable hardware ID for advertising use. Declare ads purposes in Data safety. |
| Non-ad analytics across apps from one developer | Consider App Set ID if it is not used for advertising; use FID for app-instance-level analytics. | App Set ID cannot be used for ads personalization/measurement. FID/GUID are Android/Firebase implementation suggestions and still require disclosure and SDK review. |
| Crash reporting, performance, testing, cross-device app installations, and preferences | FID or App Set ID for crash reporting/testing; Firebase Performance Monitoring for performance; FID/GUID for app-install identity across devices; FID/GUID for signed-out preferences, avoiding persistence across reinstalls. | Scenario-matched engineering guidance, not a required Play SDK list. Resetting preferences on reinstall may match user expectations. |
| Backend abuse detection and DRM | Use a Play Integrity token for integrity signals and DRM APIs for content protection. | Integrity and DRM are specialized APIs, not cross-app advertising IDs, and do not replace User Data/Data safety disclosures. |

## Pre-release checks

1. Inspect the AAB, dependency tree, and final merged manifest for AAID SDKs, AD_ID, and variant differences.
2. Test SDK behavior for opt-out, reset, deletion, no ID, no Play services, and child/unknown-age audiences.
3. Verify data pipelines do not create prohibited AAID-to-PII/persistent-ID joins, and that old ID-derived data cannot be recovered across a reset or deletion.
4. Reconcile the privacy policy, in-app disclosure, Data safety, and actual SDK/ad-mediation network behavior.
5. Retain SDK versions, permission results, consent states, test evidence, and Play Console notices for review or appeal.
