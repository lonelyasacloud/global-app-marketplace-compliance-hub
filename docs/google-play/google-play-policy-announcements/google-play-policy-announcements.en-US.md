## All announcements (newest first)

1. **2026-07-15 — [Policy announcement](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17134731):** Tightens requirements for anonymous/random chat and bars anonymous chat apps from targeting children. Phone-call verification is no longer an allowed `READ_CALL_LOG` use case; the notice suggests alternatives including the Digital Credentials API and SMS Retriever. Play apps must complete developer verification registration. It also clarifies earned wage access, third-party AI data handling, content ratings, and location disclosures, and says standardized numbering is rolling out across some policies. The notice's “policies” link points to the Functionality/Content/Experience article, which currently numbers sections 1 and 2 but does not provide a cross-policy numbering catalog. Linked resources include developer verification and Play Console requirements, PolicyBytes, Play Academy’s privacy-policy best-practices video, and Android Skills.

Recommends the location button for precise location, directs geofencing use cases to the Geofence API, and clarifies photo/video permissions, health data, and age-gating for incidental dating features. Linked resources include Health Connect guidance, news/magazine declarations, the prediction-market pilot (the notice’s June 1, 2026 deadline has passed), PolicyBytes, and the developer community.

## Best-practice and practical child links

These resources were explicitly recommended by announcements or are direct practical guides in their link trail. Apply them within their stated scope; the policy text takes precedence over a guide summary.

### Publishing, white-label apps, and store listings

- [Best Practices for White Label Developers](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15884185): Google strongly recommends decentralized accounts per client/client group (managed or unmanaged), but this is not a universal account mandate. Each template app should have a unique listing, accurate reproducible functionality claims, appropriate screenshots, and transparent audience information. Reviews are typically completed within 7 days but can take longer; Managed publishing controls approved updates for already-published apps, not first publication. See the [full inventory of 11 body text links and four image assets](../google-play-announcement-resources/google-play-announcement-resources.en-US.md).
- [Helpful tips to get your app published on Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15191715): Explain the app’s value and real functionality; ensure its title, description, and screenshots match the app; provide valid reviewer sign-in details; test installation, loading, and stability using test tracks; and fix the cause of a rejection before resubmitting.
- [Best practices for your store listing](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13393723): Keep metadata concise, accurate, and original. Avoid keyword stuffing, unattributed testimonials, misleading ranking/discount/affiliation claims, and assets that misrepresent functionality. Make icons and screenshots reflect the actual app across device sizes and supported languages. The guide links to app-description, graphics, and Play Academy training resources.
- [Play Console Requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10788890): Referenced by announcements and the white-label guide. For functionality behind a sign-in wall, provide reviewers working credentials, access instructions, and any required resources. See the detailed [Requirements for providing sign in details for review](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15748846).

### AI, child safety, privacy, and SDKs

- [Best Practices to Safeguard AI-Generated Content](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16353813): Developers remain responsible for generative-AI output. Safeguards can include filters/classifiers for harmful inputs and outputs, plus safety testing and red teaming aligned with industry standards. The guide links Google’s Secure AI Framework (SAIF), OWASP’s GenAI Red Teaming Guide, and safety resources from model providers.
- [Developer good practices: Combating online child sexual exploitation and abuse](https://technologycoalition.org/resources/developer-good-practices-combating-online-child-sexual-exploitation-and-abuse/): Tech Coalition guidance on child sexual exploitation and abuse, including public terms/community standards and internal identification and response processes. Read it alongside Play Child Safety Standards self-certification.
- [Build user trust with an accurate privacy policy](https://playacademy.exceedlms.com/student/activity/1961002-build-user-trust-with-an-accurate-privacy-policy): Play Academy video course cited by the announcement; its public description points to checks before submitting the Data safety form and lists June 18, 2026, 2 minutes, Beginner. The video requires sign-in; binding duties remain in User Data and Data safety.
- [Play Academy: SDK Best Practices](https://g.co/playacademy/sdkbestpractices): SDK-safety training recommended in the 2023 announcement. Also review [SDK Requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13323374): app developers remain responsible for the data practices of third-party SDKs they integrate.

### Policy learning and pre-submission checks

- [PolicyBytes Hub](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16373081): Index of videos in which Google Play team members explain policy updates. Use it to understand context, then verify the written policy and current dates.
- [Google Play Developer Help Community](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/community): Ask questions, find common publishing/policy issues, and exchange experience with developers and Product Experts.
- [Android Skills — Google Play policy skill](https://github.com/android/skills): Open-source tooling recommended in the July 2026 announcement. It grounds an assistant in Google Play policies for code checks in an IDE/CLI; automated results are pre-submission guidance, not a substitute for policy text or Play’s review decision.
