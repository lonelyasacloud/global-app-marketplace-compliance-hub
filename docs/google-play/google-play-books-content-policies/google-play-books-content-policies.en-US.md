## Content requirements

- **Spam, deception, and low quality:** No promotional/commercial abuse, malware, metadata that impersonates another work or misstates its format, gibberish or repetitive content with no narrative or educational value, or files with severe formatting defects or unreadable text. Reposting freely available material without meaningful depth or curation may fail quality requirements.
- **Public-domain and duplicate submissions:** Because widespread duplication confuses users, Google generally no longer accepts public-domain material except from select partners; the public policy does not provide a public eligibility list. Google does not mediate rights conflicts among authors and publishers supplying the same non-exclusive work. If it cannot confirm that the account is authorized to upload the content, the account may also be disapproved.
- **Violence, hate, and self-harm:** Prohibited content includes incitement to violence or terrorism, serious threats, extremely graphic violence or scatology, and encouragement of suicide, self-harm, eating disorders, or conduct likely to cause serious injury or death. Content promoting hate, violence, or genocide against an identifiable or protected group or individual is prohibited.
- **Sexual content and mature-audience labeling:** Nudity or sexual content with artistic, educational, historical, or documentary value may be allowed, but publishers must mark the book for mature audiences in its settings. Google may disable previews or limit discovery/access for underage users. Pornography, nudity/explicit audio without relevant value, explicit sexual metadata, child sexual exploitation, and extreme sexual acts such as bestiality are prohibited. The separate Publisher Program Policies page also says that, where local law permits, certain sexually suggestive nudity without artistic/educational/political purpose must have a 0% preview and a price of at least US$1 or local equivalent; recheck the current page and regional rules before submission.
- **Child safety, privacy, and illegal activity:** Google has zero tolerance for child sexual abuse text, imagery, and audio and may work with law enforcement. Unauthorized sale or distribution of private/confidential information and illegal activity are prohibited.
- **Copyright and trademarks:** Publishers must respect copyright and trademark rights. Google responds to clear infringement notices; repeated infringement may terminate an account. Google's DMCA article explains copyright notices, counter-notices, and repeat-infringer handling; this library does not determine ownership in an individual dispute.
- **Fraud and abuse:** Publishers may not manipulate book purchases or access through robots, generated search requests, fraudulent software, payment cards, gift cards, or other invalid means.

## Review and enforcement

Google combines automated tools and human review and may consider titles, covers, descriptions, account details, book contents, and reports. Possible actions include removing content or limiting visibility, disabling a preview, disabling access to Google products, deleting an account, reporting illegal material to law enforcement, debiting refunds, or suspending payments. A book under review may temporarily lose its preview.

If a publisher believes an action was mistaken, it should follow the notice's appeal instructions. The notice explains the reason and available options; an appeal may not be available for every action, and typically only one appeal may be submitted per book or account action. Publishers covered by the EU Digital Services Act may have a certified out-of-court dispute-resolution option; check the notice, current law, and applicable region.

### Audiobook and auto-narration implementation branch

The audiobook automated-fetch article is a second-generation child example: beyond audio formats and filenames, it links ebook fetching instructions, ONIX 2.1/3, CSV, currency conversion, and account-created `GGKEY` identifiers. Full ONIX 2.1/3.0/3.1 field guides were opened; because the catalogs and code lists are large and changeable, the knowledge base summarizes them by impact. Before publishing, validate stable identifiers, ISBN, title, author, format, date, and supply/pricing; align rights, supply, and price territory intersections and match price types to Partner Center settings. The ONIX troubleshooting guide treats missing sale-critical data as Errors and missing category/metadata data as Warnings; “warning” does not mean fully featured or error-free. The eISBN and Rights-feed descendants were opened; the sample XML download could not be parsed by the browser.

In the editor, all text in the text box is converted to speech except struck-through text. Find/replace can correct recurring words, and Ctrl+F moves through matches. Review the final spoken text before generation so that required passages are not accidentally deleted and unwanted passages do not remain.

### New books, pre-orders, audio quality, and series

The [quickstart guide](https://support.google.com/books/partner/answer/9261664?hl=en) walks through metadata, type/genre, contributors, series, settings, files, pricing, review, and publishing. Use an ISBN/EAN or a GGKEY assigned by Partner Center. A title and at least one genre are baseline publication fields; Google recommends no more than three genres, all from one subject standard. Audiobooks require a narrator; AI-generated audiobooks should use “Synthesized voice.” The ordinary audiobook sample setting defaults to 10%, with a 1-minute minimum and 30-minute maximum. This configurable default is distinct from the runtime-based preview rules for auto-narrated audiobooks. The guide says a book is usually published within 24 hours after Review/Publish; the account processing status is authoritative.

[Book file guidelines](https://support.google.com/books/partner/answer/3424254?hl=en) add audiobook constraints: duration from 5 minutes to 100 hours; MP3 and AAC/M4A preferably constant-bitrate, at least 128 kbps mono or 256 kbps stereo; FLAC and PCM WAV at 16 bits and at least 44.1 kHz. Covers must be JPG/PNG, at least 72 dpi, 1,024–7,200 pixels in each dimension, with square proportions recommended. Each audiobook can have one PDF supplement under 100 MB. For bulk upload, filenames based on ISBN/GGKEY and XofY are recommended; other patterns may add a 24-hour processing delay. These are publishing/file operations, not content-admission rules.

[DRM options](https://support.google.com/books/partner/answer/3309439?hl=en) explain the purpose of Digital Rights Management and how to disable it. Without DRM, purchasers have more flexibility with downloaded files. Protected downloads use Adobe Content Server 4 and require one-time device authentication. Due to technical limits, processed DRM-enabled books over 128 MB cannot be downloaded. DRM is a book-level rights/access setting and does not replace the publisher’s obligation to hold the necessary content rights.

[Enable pre-orders](https://support.google.com/books/partner/answer/2364635?hl=en) and the supported-country table create a time-bound publishing workflow. Pre-orders can be scheduled up to two years ahead. They are unavailable in India, where the book becomes available the day after release. More than seven days before release, content can initially be absent; within the seven-day window, content must be supplied, and the full content is due no later than three days before release or pre-orders may be cancelled. New pre-orders cannot be created within three days of release, and metadata/pricing changes lock at that point. Audiobooks need a cover before pre-order. Buyers are charged on release; analytics use the order date, while earnings reflect the book after digital delivery. If the price falls after an order, the buyer pays the lower of the two prices. These are publisher setup, processing, and reporting points—not Android app-policy deadlines.

[Audiobook sound quality issues](https://support.google.com/books/partner/answer/9550388?hl=en) says newly uploaded audio is checked for anomalies/skips, repeated patterns, long silence, and test tones. Publishers can listen and mark an issue as an error or as intentional/correct. To fix an error, upload a complete replacement file set for another review. Once all errors are fixed or dismissed, the system automatically makes the audiobook Live. This release-quality check runs alongside content-policy review.

[Get started with series](https://support.google.com/books/partner/answer/11069638?hl=en) defines a series as books intended to be read or listened to in order. The series name must match exactly across titles, including capitalization, spaces, and punctuation. Sequence numbers use whole Arabic numerals with no gaps or duplicates, subject to exceptions for certain unnumbered special types. Complete series/book metadata, book type, language, and genre choices for comics/light novels affect series presentation; updates can take up to 48 hours. The [Series catalog browse/search guide](https://support.google.com/books/partner/answer/13160776?hl=en) describes status sorting and case-insensitive partial-name search.

[Series bundle discounts](https://support.google.com/books/partner/answer/11098073?hl=en) are a promotion feature, not an exception to content or rights rules. Eligibility requires a live series, complete metadata and unique consecutive numbering, at least two books (pre-orders do not count), an average book price below US$20, the same Partner Center account, and one series. Ebooks and audiobooks qualify. A campaign can have up to three discount tiers, and the discount must increase with the number of books purchased. Details cannot be edited after creation, although campaigns can be paused or resumed; metadata changes can take about 24 hours to propagate. A multi-series promotion still cannot combine books from different series into one series bundle.

[Custom audiobook table of contents](https://support.google.com/books/partner/answer/17017903?hl=en) points to the external [W3C Audiobooks Recommendation](https://www.w3.org/TR/audiobooks/), which is a technical standard, not Play policy. The workflow creates toc.html and publication.json, declares the TOC, resources, conformance, and readingOrder, packages these with the audio in a ZIP, renames the extension to LPF, and uploads it. TOC links are relative paths; typically each audio file has one entry, though multiple entries may point into one file. The W3C Recommendation was published on November 10, 2020 and describes manifests, reading order, navigation, packaging, and accessibility. Example links resolve within that same specification. Operating-system ZIP-help links are general file-packaging guidance and are not treated as Play requirements.

## Pre-publication checks

- [ ] Confirm the rights chain, authorized territories/terms/formats, and submitting account; retain verifiable evidence.
- [ ] Review the text, cover, title, author, description, and preview/audio sample; do not misstate the work's format or content.
- [ ] For mature material, set the audience label, preview limit, and region/age availability correctly; the label does not exempt prohibited pornography.
- [ ] Check copyright/trademark, child safety, personal information, illegal content, and purchase-manipulation risks.
- [ ] Appeal with verifiable information through the notice and confirm whether the action is appealable and whether its one appeal has already been used.
