**Reviewed October 22, 2026.** The standalone [Misrepresentation Help article](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888689?hl=en) and the Misrepresentation section of the [consolidated Developer Program Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18258653?hl=en) effective September 30, 2026 have matching wording. The hub copy of that consolidated policy is [Developer Program Policy](../developer-program-policy/developer-program-policy.en-US.md). Google Play prohibits apps or developer accounts from impersonating others or misrepresenting/concealing ownership or primary purpose. It also prohibits coordinated activity that misleads users, including misrepresenting or concealing country of origin while directing content to users in another country, and coordinating with apps, sites, developers, or accounts to hide identity or material details when content relates to politics, social issues, or matters of public concern.

## Release review

- Developer-account names, profile details, individual/organization identity, and country of origin should be accurate; do not imply a government, official, brand, or organizational relationship that does not exist.
- The app name, icon, description, screenshots, first-run experience, and actual core functionality should align and clearly show who publishes the product and what it primarily does.
- If content, functionality, or promotion differs by country or region, check that the listing accurately describes the intended markets and content origin. Do not use a network of accounts or apps to hide the true publisher or origin.
- For content about politics, social issues, or matters of public concern, review whether related apps, sites, developers, and accounts coordinate to conceal identity or material details.

## Relationship to Impersonation

This policy overlaps with [Impersonation](../impersonation/impersonation.en-US.md): both require truthful identity, ownership, app purpose, and user-facing information. Misrepresentation explicitly calls out coordinated deception, country of origin and cross-border targeting, and concealing app/developer identity around matters of public concern. The Impersonation topic further covers false affiliation in store presentation, Android brand use, and copyright/trademark reporting routes. Review both; neither page fully replaces the other.

It is also a sibling of [Deceptive Behavior](../deceptive-behavior/deceptive-behavior.en-US.md), which focuses more on the actual experience, device-setting changes, hidden or review-only features, manipulated media, and dishonest tools. Cross-check both topics for store presentation and release identity issues.
