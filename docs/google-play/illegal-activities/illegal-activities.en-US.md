**Reviewed October 22, 2026.** The official rule is short and broad: Google Play does not allow apps that facilitate or promote illegal activities. The page gives three common examples:

- Facilitating the sale or purchase of illegal drugs.
- Depicting or encouraging minors to use or sell drugs, alcohol, or tobacco.
- Instructions for growing or manufacturing illegal drugs.

These are examples, not an exhaustive list. Review app functionality, content, catalogs, ads, and transaction flows for facilitation of unlawful activity, and confirm applicable law in every target market. The absence of an activity from these three examples does not establish permission, and legality in one region does not create worldwide permission. This article does not define a complete catalog of illegal conduct, regional exceptions, or a general declaration workflow.

## Implementation review

1. Inspect search, catalog, cart, booking, payment, delivery/pickup, and external-store links for actual assistance with illegal transactions.
2. For drug-, alcohol-, and tobacco-related content, verify age controls, target markets, and presentation/advertising context; also apply the [Inappropriate Content policy](../inappropriate-content/inappropriate-content.en-US.md).
3. Separately review gambling, financial, health, and crypto features under their specific Play policies and local regulators; this short article does not replace those rules.
4. Where the applicable rule or law is unclear, record the market, feature, and source evidence, then obtain local legal and Play Console support guidance before release.
