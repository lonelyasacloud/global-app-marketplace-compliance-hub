**Reviewed October 22, 2026.** Google's Mobile Unwanted Software (MUwS) policy applies its unwanted-software principles to the Android/Google Play ecosystem. It covers apps, binary code, framework modifications, and included SDKs. Software need not be a conventional virus: deception, stealth installation, hidden core functionality, insecure data handling, unexpected system effects, ad fraud, or interference with the user experience can also raise policy concerns.

## General MUwS baseline

- **Deliver what was promised and explain behavior.** Apps should provide the functionality presented to users. Clearly disclose core purposes, bundled software, significant installation options, and system-setting changes; let users review and approve them.
- **Do not falsify device state or identity.** Do not use fake virus, system-failure, or security alerts to induce actions; impersonate another app/developer/entity; or make ads look like OS notifications.
- **Protect user data.** Disclose access, collection, use, and sharing; keep handling within reasonably expected, policy-compliant functionality; and secure the data. The app developer remains responsible for third-party SDK/code behavior.
- **Do not disrupt the device or other apps.** Do not interfere with normal device functions, redirect network traffic, suppress other apps' or OS security alerts, evade Play Protect, or weaken protections without the user's explicit informed direction.
- **Keep ads identifiable, dismissible, and based on genuine user intent.** Unexpected pop-ups, overlays, false close buttons, system-notification imitation, out-of-app ads, and other layouts that interrupt use or cause accidental clicks are prohibited or high risk.
- **Keep install, update, and removal user-controlled.** Do not silently download/install extra software, hide behavior, or conceal bundles. Uninstall should be clear and straightforward; do not deter removal with scare messages, fees, or a “hide instead of remove” default.

Google's general [Unwanted Software Policy](https://www.google.com/about/unwanted-software-policy.html) and [Software Principles](https://www.google.com/about/software-principles.html) provide background principles. The Google Play MUwS page is the direct source for Play distribution decisions. The linked Android.com MUwS page returned a cache miss in this review; its unread contents are not treated as additional Play requirements.

## Five Play policy branches

### 1. Hostile Downloaders

Code may be treated as a hostile downloader even if it is not itself unwanted software when it spreads other MUwS. The Help article lists two signals: reason to believe the code was made to spread MUwS and it has downloaded MUwS or contains code that could download/install apps; or at least **5%** of observed app downloads are MUwS, with a minimum sample of **500 observed downloads** (at least 25 MUwS downloads). The narrow browser/file-sharing clarification applies only when the app does not drive downloads and each software download is initiated by a consenting user.

### 2. Ad Fraud

Do not generate ad interactions that trick an ad network into believing traffic reflects genuine user interest. Examples include hidden/invisible ads, automatic or non-human clicks, fake install attribution, ads popping up outside the app interface, and false claims about device type, package name, or ad inventory. Integrating an ad SDK does not remove the developer's responsibility for implementation, actual traffic, and app-identity signals.

### 3. Unauthorized Use or Imitation of System Functionality

Apps and ads must not mimic or interfere with operating-system notifications or warnings. System-level notifications may be used only for integral app features and should clearly identify their source. Do not use OS notifications to promote unrelated apps or third-party services, or make alerts appear to come from the operating system.

### 4. Social Engineering

Apps must not pretend to be another app in order to deceive users into performing actions they intended for the original trusted app. The Help entry provides only this concise rule, without detailed examples. As a release review, inspect names, icons, interface, notifications, deep links, and key action flows for cases where a user's expected destination is replaced by another app.

### 5. Interactions with other Play policies

The MUwS article explicitly requires compliance with [User Data](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311?hl=en), [Sensitive Permissions and APIs](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16558241?hl=en), [Spyware](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14745000?hl=en), and [SDK Requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13323374?hl=en). Ads also fall under the [Ads policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753?hl=en). See this library's [User Data](../user-data/user-data.en-US.md), [Sensitive Permissions](../sensitive-permissions/sensitive-permissions.en-US.md), [Spyware](../spyware-and-data-exfiltration/spyware-and-data-exfiltration.en-US.md), third-party SDK ([Google Play third-party SDK requirements and safe integration](../sdk-requirements/sdk-requirements.en-US.md)), and [Ads](../ads-and-advertising/ads-and-advertising.en-US.md) topics.

## Ad Fraud: invalid traffic and implementation best practices

Play's [Ad Fraud policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9969955?hl=en) defines fraud as ad interactions that trick a network into believing traffic reflects genuine user interest. Examples include hidden ads, automatic/bot clicks, fake install attribution, out-of-app pop-ups, and false app identity. Its linked AdMob [Invalid traffic](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/3342054?hl=en) guidance covers deliberate and accidental clicks/impressions, including publishers clicking live ads, repeated or encouraged clicks, deceptive/accidental placements, and bot traffic. Publishers remain responsible even when a third party generates invalid traffic without their knowledge; high invalid traffic may limit ad serving, suspend, or disable an AdMob account. AdMob monetization-account actions differ from Play app-distribution enforcement.

The opened [How you can prevent invalid activity](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/3342099?hl=en) article recommends:

1. Use Google test ads or test devices in development; never click live ads in your own app. The current [Android test ads guide](https://developers.google.com/admob/android/next-gen/test-ads) and [iOS test ads guide](https://developers.google.com/admob/ios/test-ads) cover demo units, test devices, and mediation testing. The Android page reports an October 20, 2026 update; iOS simulators are automatically test devices.
2. Segment traffic reports by app, ad unit, and country; monitor source and user-behavior changes and investigate anomalies.
3. Avoid low-quality ad networks or app-promotion partners that may introduce invalid traffic.
4. Follow AdMob's [Implementation guidance](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/2936217?hl=en), [Banner ad guidance](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6128877?hl=en), and [Interstitial ad guidance](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6066980?hl=en): use natural transition points, reserve stable ad space, avoid placing ads next to interactive controls/content, prevent induced or accidental clicks, and choose the right format/placement for the device.
5. Google's [Ad Traffic Quality Resource Center](https://www.google.com/ads/adtrafficquality/) describes invalid-traffic detection and defenses. It and the AdMob implementation articles are ecosystem best practices, not a separate Play Console declaration form.

Test-ad and traffic-quality guidance helps implement the policy. AdMob invalid-traffic account action and Play app-policy enforcement are related but distinct processes.

### Ad placement guidance: distinguish policy from implementation advice

AdMob's implementation Help Center lists eight related guides: [Implementation](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/2936217?hl=en), [Banner](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6128877?hl=en), [Discouraged banner implementations](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6275345?hl=en&ref_topic=2936214), [Recommended banner implementations](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6275335?hl=en&ref_topic=2936214), [300x250 banner](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6293636?hl=en&ref_topic=2936214), [Interstitial](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6066980?hl=en), [Disallowed interstitial implementations](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6201362), and [Recommended interstitial implementations](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6201350). All eight bodies have now been opened. The [recommended interstitial guide](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6201350) recommends placing interstitials at natural breaks, preferably before a break page rather than after a user taps Continue; it also suggests a short pause after high-frequency tapping and accounting for ad latency/preloading. Its direct Android/iOS SDK guides were also opened and summarized; SDK samples/API descendants remain selectively followed. This is AdMob placement guidance, not an additional Play Ads rule.

- **Banner ads:** Recommendations include clear separation from interactive controls/content, a non-clickable border, reserving a fixed ad area so late loading does not shift app content, and adaptive banners for different screen sizes. AdMob identifies proximity to controls as a high accidental-click risk. Floating over content or scrolling over a menu is explicitly labeled against AdMob policy. The 300x250 guide also prohibits ads that imitate or cover app content, appear on dead-end screens, or sit beside navigation, chats, galleries, gameplay controls, or custom close buttons. It distinguishes discouraged examples from those expressly against policy; discouraged placement can still cause invalid traffic or limited ad serving.
- **Interstitial ads:** Do not show interstitials at app launch or exit; place them at natural breaks between content. AdMob also says to show at most one interstitial after every two in-app user actions, not after every click/swipe or immediately after another interstitial, and not to block core content or navigation. [App open ads](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/9620632) are a separate format designed for app launch/return, not a general exception to interstitial rules. Check applicable AdMob and Play policies; apps that comply with the Families Policy are not eligible to use app open ads.
- **Technical implementation:** The [Android banner SDK guide](https://developers.google.com/admob/android/banner) and [Android](https://developers.google.com/admob/android/interstitial)/[iOS](https://developers.google.com/admob/ios/interstitial) interstitial SDK guides were opened; they cover test ads, load timing, and ad-format implementation. These technical documents include many SDK/API references, code samples, platform branches, and navigation links. This topic does not reproduce code or claim that technical grandchildren are exhaustive; consult official documentation for the SDK version being shipped.
- **Videos and unread targets:** Links to [banner best practices](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yTnqcz6RJ-4), [interstitial best practices](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ccrj8D0m1KA), [the banner-overlap violation explanation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KsPhOYShSPw), and [the unexpected-interstitial violation explanation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C5FW0lRAT3k) are recorded, but automated retrieval failed. ## Release checklist

1. Inventory app code, SDKs, remote code, downloaders/updaters, ad partners, and backend flows for silent installs, bundles, or unexpected actions.
2. Inspect names, icons, notifications, OS prompts, loading and exit flows for deception, fake system alerts, fake virus warnings, and impersonation of trusted apps.
3. Reconcile user-data/permission purposes, disclosure/consent, SDK behavior, privacy-policy content, and Data safety; never induce users to disable Play Protect.
4. Ensure ads are visible, in-app, dismissible, and not placed for accidental clicks; all clicks and install attributions should result from genuine user intent.
5. Use test ads during development; monitor traffic by source, country, and ad unit; review downloader and ad-SDK supply chains.
