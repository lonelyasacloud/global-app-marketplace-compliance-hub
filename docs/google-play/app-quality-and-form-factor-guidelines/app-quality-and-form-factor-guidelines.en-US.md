## Play Help: “Ensuring app quality”

The [Ensuring app quality](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13965279?hl=en) article is now open and reviewed. It links app quality to user satisfaction, downloads, retention, and security. Its practical guidance is to test across devices and operating-system versions, keep the interface intuitive, respond to user feedback, ship security and feature updates, and comply with applicable law and Play policy. It is an overview, not a new standalone policy.

### Four Android quality pillars: practical guidance

The summaries below come from [Android App quality](https://developer.android.com/quality) and its four pillar pages: [Core value](https://developer.android.com/quality/core-value), [User experience](https://developer.android.com/quality/user-experience), [Technical quality](https://developer.android.com/quality/technical), and [Design for Safety](https://developer.android.com/quality/privacy-and-security). The pages link to extensive API, device, and testing branches. This table focuses on principles relevant to Play review, user experience, and release risk; it does not turn engineering recommendations into mandatory policy.

| Pillar | Guidance summary | Related knowledge-base pages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core value | Solve a real need or provide enjoyment for the intended audience. Match feature depth to the use case without adding clutter, stale content, or bugs. Keep store assets accurate. Use ratings, engagement, and retention to evaluate delivered value; acquisition alone is not a measure of the app’s underlying value. | [Store listing best practices](../store-listing-best-practices/store-listing-best-practices.en-US.md), [Ratings and reviews](../user-ratings-reviews-installs/user-ratings-reviews-installs.en-US.md), the app-discovery section in this topic. |
| User experience | Use familiar, consistent controls; adapt to supported screens, postures, and form factors; address accessibility, localization, onboarding, sharing/backup, and deep links. Set billing expectations before install. Keep ads proportionate in duration, frequency, and placement, reduce accidental taps, and remain responsible for SDK-delivered ad content. | The form-factor section in this topic, [Payments and subscriptions](../payments/payments-and-subscriptions.en-US.md), [Ads](../ads-and-advertising/ads-and-advertising.en-US.md), [Audience and child safety](../age-restricted-child-safety/age-restricted-child-safety.en-US.md). |
| Technical quality | Monitor crashes, ANRs, low-memory kills, startup, rendering, battery, network, and app size across devices. Android Vitals/Reporting API can surface device differences; staged testing, rollout, and monitoring help contain production regressions. Frame-rate values and optimization techniques in the guide are experience recommendations, not universal Play policy thresholds. | The Android Vitals section in this topic, [Testing and release workflow](../publishing/play-release-workflow.en-US.md). |
| Privacy and security | Minimize permissions, location precision/frequency, installed-app visibility, and non-resettable identifiers. Prefer system pickers, app-specific storage, and companion-device APIs. Request access in context, track permission use, respect denials, protect data in transit and at rest, and manage authentication, integrity, and SDK dependencies. | [User Data](../user-data/user-data.en-US.md), [Android privacy checklist](../android-privacy-checklist/android-privacy-checklist.en-US.md), [Sensitive Permissions](../sensitive-permissions/sensitive-permissions.en-US.md), [Third-party SDKs](../sdk-requirements/sdk-requirements.en-US.md). |

**Boundary:** The Play Help article links to five policy bodies; those policies define obligations. The Android pillar pages provide engineering guidance, and do not make each recommendation a review condition. For example, frame-rate targets and optimization techniques are not universal violation thresholds. Technical/API descendants remain partly open; follow the relevant topic and official source for the app’s feature set.

### Adaptive app quality: October 21, 2026 update, tiers, and test matrix

Reopened Android Developers' [Adaptive app quality guidelines](https://developer.android.com/docs/quality-guidelines/adaptive-app-quality) on October 24, 2026; the page says it was last updated October 21. It covers phones, tablets, foldables, desktops, car displays, TVs, and XR, as well as multi-window, multi-display, multi-instance, picture-in-picture, and foldable postures. It points developers to compatibility checklists and tests but explicitly says that most apps do not need every requirement: implement the checks that fit the app's use cases.

| Tier | Page summary | How to apply it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 3 — Adaptive ready | Runs full-screen or in the full available window across devices; is not letterboxed or in compatibility mode; critical flows work; basic keyboard, mouse, trackpad, and stylus input works. | Establish usable layouts and working critical flows; the layout may not yet be optimal. |
| Tier 2 — Adaptive optimized | Adds responsive/adaptive layouts for screen sizes and device configurations and enhanced external-input support. | The guide recommends Tier 2 for an excellent experience across Android devices. |
| Tier 1 — Adaptive differentiated | Designs the experience for the device/display and, where applicable, supports multitasking, foldable postures, drag and drop, and stylus. | Use for differentiated foldable and large-screen/desktop experiences; not every app must implement every capability. |

The current compatibility test set recommends at least: Googlebook (160 ppi), foldable (841×701 dp), 8-inch tablet (1024×640 dp), 10.5-inch tablet (1280×800 dp), and 13-inch Chromebook (1600×900 dp). Suggested emulators are Pixel Fold, Medium Tablet, and Desktop (Preview). The Desktop Preview emulator requires Android Studio Canary and the preview-install instructions; the page also links to a resizable emulator for checking multiple window sizes. This is engineering validation guidance, not a universal Play review obligation.

### Implicit intent → contact-selection descendants

Core quality's contact alternative links to [Common intents](https://developer.android.com/guide/components/intents-common), specifically its Contacts/people section. That section distinguishes user selection from an app querying all or specific contact data:

- Use `ACTION_PICK` with `Contacts.CONTENT_TYPE`; after the user chooses a contact, the system returns a `content:` URI and grants temporary access. The guide says `READ_CONTACTS` is generally unnecessary for basic details such as display name.
- When only one field is needed, specify a narrower MIME type such as `CommonDataKinds.Phone.CONTENT_TYPE`. Reading specific phone numbers or email addresses may still require `READ_CONTACTS`; a picker is not permission to read the whole contacts database.
- The [legacy Contacts Provider link](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/providers/contacts-provider) redirects to the [current Contacts Provider guide](https://developer.android.com/identity/providers/contacts-provider). This pass checked its table structure, selected-URI relationship, and `ContactsContract`/Content Provider descendant entry points. Its 1,368-line sync-adapter, write, account, and API-reference material was not fully reviewed.

## Android vitals: post-release quality signals and discoverability

**Reviewed October 22, 2026.** The [Android vitals Help article](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9844486?hl=en) is a post-release quality-monitoring and improvement surface, not another content policy. It says that exceeding bad-behavior thresholds on core stability/battery metrics may reduce Play discoverability; users on specific device models may be steered toward other apps, and a store-listing warning may appear. Play generally evaluates the most recent 28 days and may act sooner after a spike. Values below are the page snapshot on the review date; thresholds may change, so recheck the live Help page and Play Console before release.

### Data scope and interpretation

- Play Console shows the previous 90 days; the [Play Developer Reporting API](https://developers.google.com/play/developer/reporting) provides up to three years of reporting history and supports internal analysis and automated workflows. Android vitals updates daily; no data usually means the selected filters do not have enough samples.
- Data comes from opted-in users on a subset of devices that share usage and diagnostics data. Technical issues on app versions not installed through Google Play and uncertified device models are excluded from these Android vitals metrics. Users can review or change their device setting in [Google Account Help](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/6078260).
- General dimensions include app version, Android version, form factor, device model, and country/region. Some metrics can also be broken down by wake-lock name, alarm wake-up name, ANR activity, and ANR type. A missing or healthy aggregate does not rule out a concentrated issue on a small set of devices or versions.

### Core thresholds explicitly listed by Play

| Metric | Threshold shown on the October 22, 2026 page | Play impact described by the page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| User-perceived ANR | At least **0.47%** of daily active users across all devices; at least **8%** for a single device model | Exceeding the threshold counts as bad behavior and may reduce discoverability; steering may target a device model. |
| User-perceived crash | At least **1.09%** of daily users across all devices; at least **8%** for a single device model | Same discoverability impact; fix high-impact clusters on the Crashes and ANRs page first. |
| Excessive watch-face battery use | More than **1%** of watch-face sessions overall or on one device model | This is watch-face-specific; do not generalize it to phone apps. |
| Excessive partial wake locks (beta) | More than **5%** of battery sessions include partial wake locks totaling over **3 hours** | Listed among core metrics, but the Help page explicitly says that exceeding this beta threshold does **not currently** reduce Play discoverability. |

The separate “core value” section describes two more signals that may trigger a listing warning or affect eligibility for some Play surfaces: DAU/MAU below the **8%** threshold, and a user-loss rate above **5%**. These are discoverability/surface mechanisms described by the page, not content-policy violations or account sanctions.

### Other quality diagnostics

Android vitals also reports ANR, crash, low-memory-kill, start-up-time, game frame-rate, UI jank/frozen-frame, wake-lock, alarm wake-up, background Wi-Fi/network-use, and permission-denial metrics. Its “slow start” cutoffs are cold start ≥5 seconds, warm ≥2 seconds, and hot ≥1 second. For games, slow-session metrics count sessions where more than 25% of frames miss the selected 30/20 FPS benchmark; the page recommends targeting 30 FPS for most games, and at least 75% of frames at 20 FPS where lower rates are needed. UI Toolkit metrics use the 16 ms frame deadline and 700 ms frozen-frame definition to diagnose user-visible issues. These are measurement definitions and engineering guidance; they are not all mandatory Play thresholds for every app.

The Help article gives triggers for several background metrics: stuck partial wake locks over one hour; more than 10 wake-ups per hour; more than four background Wi-Fi scans per hour; and more than 50 MB of background network use per day while the device is not charging. It also records watch-face battery use above 4.44% per hour while the device is not charging and no apps are in use. A separate Android watch-face battery guide recommends staying below 3.2% per hour in 80% of sessions and below 1% excessive battery usage overall; these are watch-face engineering recommendations, not requirements for ordinary phone apps. Permission-denial rates are UX diagnostics, not a reason to request more permissions.
