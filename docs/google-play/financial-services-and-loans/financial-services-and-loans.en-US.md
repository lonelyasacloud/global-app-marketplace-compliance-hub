**Reviewed October 22, 2026.** Google Play's Financial Services policy covers financial products tied to managing or investing money and crypto assets, personalized financial advice, and lending. Every app with financial features must complete the Financial features declaration in Play Console. Personal-loan, loan-facilitation, and Earned Wage Access (EWA) apps also face store-listing, licensing, sensitive-permission, and target-market requirements. A Play declaration is not a local financial license and does not prove Google has approved operation in every market.

## Financial features declaration for every app

Developers with published Google Play apps must accurately complete **App content → Financial features**. Apps with no financial feature must still select the “My app doesn’t provide any financial features” response. The requirement covers closed, open, and production tracks, as well as preloaded apps updated through Play. The Help article names qualifying system services and private apps as exceptions; an app is not exempt merely because it is preloaded or enterprise-branded.

The form's listed categories include:

| Category | Examples to declare |
| --- | --- |
| Banking and loans | Direct personal lender, loan facilitator, payday loan, banking, line of credit, earned wage access, microfinance |
| Payments and transfers | Mobile payments/digital wallets, money transfers and wire services |
| Purchase agreements | Rewards, points, miles and similar incentives; buy now, pay later (BNPL) |
| Trading and funds | Crypto wallet/exchange, NFT sales/trading/rewards, stock trading/portfolio management, crowdfunding/chit funds |
| Support services | Credit monitoring/reporting, financial advice, insurance, other |

All loan-related declarations must reflect whether the app lends directly, generates leads/facilitates, or connects users with third-party lenders. Personal-loan apps must also provide the lenders they work with and how each relates to the developer. If a target market requires additional documents, upload the valid license or evidence requested for that market. Keep declarations, store information, lender names, and actual app behavior aligned.

## Personal loans and lines of credit

Google Play defines a personal loan as a non-recurring loan from a person or entity to an individual consumer, not financing for a fixed asset or education. Help-page examples include personal, payday, peer-to-peer, and title loans; mortgages, car loans, and revolving credit lines such as credit cards and personal lines are excluded from that definition's examples. An April 2025 announcement extended the Personal Loans policy to line-of-credit apps. Current general requirements expressly include apps primarily facilitating personal loans or lines of credit in the sensitive-data restrictions. Classify the actual product and policy scope; do not decide based only on marketing terminology.

Apps that provide personal loans directly, generate leads, or connect users to third-party lenders must:

- Set the Play Store app category to **Finance**.
- Disclose the minimum/maximum repayment period, maximum APR, a representative total-cost example including principal and all applicable fees, and a privacy policy that comprehensively describes personal/sensitive-data access, collection, use, and sharing.
- Calculate APR to include interest, fees, and other annual costs, or use the comparable rate consistently with local law.
- Hold required licenses in every target market and be able to substantiate the link between the license holder and Play developer account/partners.

In general, Google Play does not allow personal loans that require full repayment **60 days or less** after issuance. A July 2025 update says only Pakistan may have a limited exception when short-term loans are explicitly required by local law/regulation. This is not a general exemption; Pakistan SECP approval and local requirements still apply.

**United States:** personal-loan apps with APR of **36% or higher** are not allowed. The maximum APR must be disclosed and calculated consistently with the Truth in Lending Act (TILA). This applies to direct lenders, lead generators, and apps connecting users to third-party lenders.

## Earned Wage Access (EWA)

The current policy describes EWA as allowing a user to access part of wages already earned in the current pay period but not yet paid. Typical policy characteristics include: access limited to wages already earned; repayment through payroll deduction or automatic bank payment, with no additional interest, penalty, or fee if the debit fails; typically no interest, with a low flat or transaction fee instead; and transactions typically not reported to credit bureaus, avoiding credit-score impact or long-term debt. The Help page gives $1–$5 per transaction or 1–5% of the advance as an illustrative range. These are policy descriptions of typical EWA products, not a legal cap or cross-market safe harbor; verify local law and fee transparency.

Direct EWA providers, lead generators, and third-party connectors must also set the Finance category and disclose repayment terms, all fees (including subscription and transaction fees), a representative total-cost example, and a comprehensive privacy policy. A July 2026 announcement reiterates that EWA must meet the same standards of transparency and privacy protection as other financial services.

## Sensitive-data and permission restrictions for loan apps

Personal-loan apps, apps primarily facilitating personal loans or lines of credit, accessory loan/credit apps such as calculators and guides, and EWA apps must not access the sensitive data covered by this rule or declare these permissions:

`READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE`, `READ_MEDIA_IMAGES`, `READ_CONTACTS`, `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION`, `READ_PHONE_NUMBERS`, `READ_MEDIA_VIDEO`, `QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES`, and `WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE`.

This sector-specific rule applies alongside the general Sensitive Permissions, User Data, privacy-policy, and Data safety requirements. A permission not named in this list is not automatically permitted; SMS/Call Log use, for example, is separately restricted by handler and use-case rules. For files or contacts a user deliberately provides, consider the [system Photo Picker](../photo-video-permissions/photo-video-permissions.en-US.md), Contact Picker, or another minimum-scope alternative, then apply all other relevant policies.

## Crypto assets, other financial features, and developer account type

The declaration also covers digital wallets, transfers, BNPL, crypto wallets/exchanges, NFTs, stock trading, crowdfunding, insurance, and more. The Financial Services policy prohibits binary-options trading. Crypto exchanges and software wallets are additionally covered by the Blockchain-based Content/crypto policy; on-device crypto mining is disallowed, while remote mining management may be considered under its separate rules; tokenized-asset sales/rewards require declaration. These are separate policy branches, not exemptions from loan rules, and require their own jurisdiction/licensing review. Real-money prediction markets have a separate, time-limited Google Play pilot; distinguish that pilot from the base Financial Services declaration and the real-money gambling route. The [Prediction Market Pilot topic](../prediction-market-pilot/prediction-market-pilot.en-US.md) covers eligibility, U.S. license scope, Organization accounts, 18+ controls, AO/IARC rating, responsible-use controls, and the passed June 1, 2026 deadline for existing apps.

Play Console requires Organization developer accounts for businesses providing financial services, among others. Match the developer's legal identity, public profile, and licensed business entity; see [Play Console Requirements](../play-console-requirements/play-console-requirements.en-US.md).

## Pre-release checklist

1. Declare every actual payment, credit, advice, trading, wallet, insurance, and reward feature; do not classify by app name or store category alone.
2. For loan/EWA/facilitation apps, verify Finance category, all partners, licenses, store terms/fee disclosures, and privacy policy.
3. Search manifests, merged manifests, SDKs, build variants, and release tracks for prohibited lending permissions; also inspect SDK/server data collection.
